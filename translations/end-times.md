# When — אַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים · συντέλεια τοῦ αἰῶνος

## Why this passage, and what kind of question it is

The earlier chapters deal with two kinds of problem. `OUR-TRANSLATION.md`
(Deut 32:8) is a **textual** one: the scrolls and the Greek read one thing, the
Masoretic text another, and three witnesses disagreeing is a manuscript fact.
`psalm-82.md` is a **grammatical** one: one stable text, but the construct chain
and the verb agreement genuinely permit more than one sense.

This chapter is a third kind, and the most common one. **The manuscripts are
not in dispute here at all.** Every verse below reads the same in every witness
this project holds. What has moved is the *English* — two ordinary words, one
Hebrew and one Greek, have been given a technical apocalyptic sense in
translation that the underlying vocabulary does not carry on its own.

That makes this the easiest kind of drift to check and the hardest kind to
notice, because there is no variant to point at. The only way to see it is to
look at where else the same word is used, and let the corpus answer.

Every count below was produced from the corpus on disk (306,785 Hebrew words,
23,213 verses; SBLGNT + MorphGNT for the Greek), not from a lexicon and not
from memory.

## Part I — the Hebrew: אַחֲרִית

### The word, and how often

**אַחֲרִית** occurs **61 times** in the Hebrew Bible. **Thirteen** of those form
the phrase בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים, commonly rendered *"in the last days"* or
*"in the latter days"*:

> Gen 49:1 · Num 24:14 · Deut 4:30 · Deut 31:29 · Isa 2:2 · Jer 23:20 ·
> Jer 30:24 · Jer 48:47 · Jer 49:39 · Ezek 38:16 · Dan 10:14 · Hos 3:5 · Mic 4:1

The remaining 48 occurrences are the evidence for what the word means.

### Word by word — Genesis 49:1, the first occurrence

**וַיִּקְרָא** `ו/יקרא` `HC/Vqw3ms` — conjunction + verb, qal, wayyiqtol, 3ms.
Root **קרא**. Range: *to call · to summon · to proclaim · to read aloud.*
**"And he called"**

**יַעֲקֹב** `יעקב` `HNp` — proper name. **"Jacob"**

**אֶל־בָּנָיו** `אל` `HR` + `בני/ו` `HNcmpc/Sp3ms` — preposition + noun, common
masc **plural construct** + suffix 3ms. **"to his sons"**

**וַיֹּאמֶר** `ו/יאמר` `HC/Vqw3ms` — conjunction + verb, qal, wayyiqtol, 3ms.
Root **אמר**. **"and he said"**

**הֵאָסְפוּ** `האספו` `HVNv2mp` — verb, niphal, imperative, 2mp. Root **אסף**.
Range: *to gather · to be gathered · to be taken in.* **"be gathered"**

**בְּאַחֲרִית** `ב/אחרית` `HR/Ncfsc` — preposition בְּ + noun, common fem sing
**construct**.
> Root **אחר** (Strong's 319) — the same root as אַחַר, *after, behind*. The
> noun names **the after-part of a thing**: its later portion, its far end, its
> outcome. It is a **positional** word, and the position can be temporal or
> spatial (see Ps 139:9 below, where it is unambiguously spatial).
> Range, all attested in the corpus: *the after-part · the latter portion ·
> the end (of a measured period) · the outcome, what a thing comes to ·
> the far side · what comes after (posterity, sequel) · a future.*
> Being **construct**, it cannot stand alone — it must bind to what follows.
> **"in the after-part of"**

**הַיָּמִים** `ה/ימים` — article + noun, common masc plural. Root **יום**,
day. Range: *days · a period of days · time generally.* **"the days"**

> **Verse:** *"And Jacob called to his sons and said: Be gathered, and I will
> tell you what will meet you in the after-part of the days."*

⚠️ **What the context is.** Jacob is dying. He gathers twelve sons and tells
each one what will become of his line. The content that follows is tribal
history — Reuben loses precedence, Judah gets the sceptre, Joseph is fruitful.
Nothing in the chapter concerns a consummation of history. The phrase here
means **later on**.

The second occurrence, **Num 24:14**, is the same. Balaam, dismissed by Balak,
says he will advise him what this people will do to *your* people בְּאַחֲרִית
הַיָּמִים — and then delivers oracles about Moab, Edom, Amalek and Asshur. Also
ordinary future history.

### The control: Deuteronomy 11:12 — identical grammar, calendar meaning

This is the strongest single piece of evidence in the chapter, because the
**word and the construction are the same** while the referent is plainly a
calendar year:

> **מֵרֵשִׁית הַשָּׁנָה וְעַד אַחֲרִית שָׁנָה**
> `אחרית` `HNcfsc` — noun, common fem sing **construct** — the same form as
> Gen 49:1, bound to שָׁנָה instead of הַיָּמִים.
>
> *"from the beginning of the year to the **after-part of** the year"*

**רֵאשִׁית ↔ אַחֲרִית** is a fixed opposition: the front-part and the
after-part. It is the ordinary way Hebrew brackets a span. The same pairing
runs through **Job 8:7** (רֵאשִׁיתְךָ … וְאַחֲרִיתְךָ, your beginning … your
latter state) and **Job 42:12**, where *YHWH blessed the אחרית of Job more than
his רֵאשִׁית* — his later life against his earlier one.

### The spatial control: Psalm 139:9

> **בְּאַחֲרִית יָם** `ב/אחרית` `HR/Ncfsc` — preposition + construct, again the
> identical form. *"in the after-part of the sea"* — the far side, the remotest
> part. **Not temporal at all.**

A word that can mean the far edge of the sea is not, by itself, a term for the
end of history.

### The outcome sense: Proverbs, Numbers, Psalms

- **Prov 14:12** — a way is straight before a man, *וְאַחֲרִיתָהּ דַּרְכֵי מָוֶת*
  — its **outcome** is the ways of death.
- **Prov 5:4** — of the strange woman, *וְאַחֲרִיתָהּ מָרָה כַלַּעֲנָה* — her
  **outcome/aftermath** is bitter as wormwood.
- **Num 23:10** — Balaam: *וּתְהִי אַחֲרִיתִי כָּמֹהוּ* — let my **end** be
  like his.
- **Ps 37:37–38** — *כִּי אַחֲרִית לְאִישׁ שָׁלוֹם* … *אַחֲרִית רְשָׁעִים
  נִכְרָתָה* — the **latter end** of the upright, of the wicked.
- **Deut 32:29** — *יָבִינוּ לְאַחֲרִיתָם* — that they would discern their
  **outcome**.

### ⭐ Jeremiah 29:11 — the same word, translated the opposite way

> **לָתֵת לָכֶם אַחֲרִית וְתִקְוָה**
> `אחרית` `HNcfsa` — noun, common fem sing **absolute**. Not construct: it
> stands alone, bound to nothing.
>
> *"to give you an after-part and a hope"*

It is the same noun as Isaiah 2:2's "latter days" — and it is familiar in
English as **"a future and a hope."**

> 🛑 **An outside claim, and this project cannot check it.** Statements in this
> chapter about how English versions *render* a word — "commonly rendered the
> last days," "familiar as a future and a hope" — are from the author's general
> knowledge. **This corpus holds no English translation to verify them against,
> deliberately** (see `PROVENANCE.md`). They are offered as orientation for the
> reader, and **no argument here rests on them.** Everything load-bearing is the
> Hebrew and Greek, which is on disk and checkable.

⚠️ **A caution about the parse codes, carried over from `genesis-1.md`.** The
morphology tags read `Ncfsc` (construct) in the thirteen phrase occurrences and
`Ncfsa` (absolute) here. That looks like a grammatical distinction, and it is
**not a form-based one**: nouns of this **-ִית** pattern *do not change shape*
between construct and absolute — the Genesis chapter established exactly this
for רֵאשִׁית and names אַחֲרִית as likewise invariant. The tag is the tagger's
judgment following the Masoretic reading, not something the letters settle.

⭐ **Which makes the point stronger, not weaker.** The written word is the same
in Isaiah 2:2 and Jeremiah 29:11. Nothing in the consonants, and nothing in the
form, marks one as eschatological and the other as ordinary. Both the
grammatical label and the English rendering are **supplied by the reader, not
by the text.**

### What Part I claims, and what it does not

**Claimed:** אַחֲרִית is a positional noun meaning the after-part, latter
portion, outcome or far end of whatever it is bound to; it is used of a year,
of a sea, of a man's life, and of days; and בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים in its first
two occurrences describes ordinary future history.

**Not claimed:** that no occurrence of the phrase is eschatological. Isa 2:2,
Dan 10:14, Ezek 38:16 and Hos 3:5 sit in contexts about the fate of nations
and a mountain of YHWH established at the head of the mountains, and a reader
may reasonably take those as far-horizon. **Both readings are printed and
neither is preferred**, per this project's rule. What the corpus establishes is
that the *phrase itself* does not carry that weight — the context does, where
it does at all.

## Part II — the Greek: συντέλεια τοῦ αἰῶνος

### Matthew 24:3 — the disciples' question, word by word

> Καθημένου δὲ αὐτοῦ ἐπὶ τοῦ Ὄρους τῶν Ἐλαιῶν προσῆλθον αὐτῷ οἱ μαθηταὶ κατʼ
> ἰδίαν λέγοντες· Εἰπὸν ἡμῖν πότε ταῦτα ἔσται καὶ τί τὸ σημεῖον τῆς σῆς
> παρουσίας καὶ συντελείας τοῦ αἰῶνος;

**πότε** · interrogative adverb. *when*

**ταῦτα ἔσται** · demonstrative pron. neut. pl. + εἰμί, fut. indic. 3sg.
*these things will be*

**τὸ σημεῖον** · article + noun, nom. sing. neut., σημεῖον
> Range: *sign · mark · token · portent.* **"the sign"**

**τῆς σῆς παρουσίας** · article + possessive adj. 2sg + noun, gen. sing. fem.,
παρουσία
> From πάρειμι, *to be present, be at hand* (παρά + εἰμί) — a morphological
> fact, checkable in the form. Range: *presence · arrival · coming · advent.*
> **"of your arrival/presence"**
> 🛑 **Outside claim, not supported from this corpus:** it is often said that
> παρουσία was the standard Hellenistic term for the ceremonial visit of a
> ruler to a city. That rests on inscriptions and papyri **this project does not
> hold**, so it is named here and **not used as evidence for anything below.**

**καὶ συντελείας** · noun, gen. sing. fem., συντέλεια
> From **σύν** (*together, with*) + **τέλος** (*end, completion, goal*). The
> compound names a **completing-together**, a bringing to full term — a
> consummation. Range: *completion · consummation · the bringing of a thing to
> its full term.*
> ⚠️ Note it is not a word for destruction. τέλος is the word for the *goal or
> finish* of a thing, not its annihilation. **"and of the completion of"**

**τοῦ αἰῶνος** · article + noun, gen. sing. masc., αἰών
> Range: *age · epoch · era · a long stretch of time · lifetime.*
> **"the age"**

> **Verse:** *"And as he was sitting on the Mount of the Olives, the disciples
> came to him privately, saying: Tell us, when will these things be, and what
> is the sign of your arrival and of the completion of the age?"*

### 🛑 The word "world" is not in this verse

I checked the verse token by token. **No form of κόσμος occurs in Matthew 24:3.**
The Greek for *world* was available — it occurs **185 times** in the NT — and it
is not what stands here.

> **A note on counting.** Every Greek figure in this chapter is a **lemma
> count from MorphGNT** (`greek-nt/morphgnt`), which tags the whole SBLGNT by
> dictionary headword. Two earlier methods failed and are worth recording:
> a naive stem search (`αιων-` also catches the separate adjective **αἰώνιος**,
> 69×; `γενε-` catches γίνομαι, γένεσις and γενεαλογία), and then a hand-built
> list of inflected forms, which still under-counted because SBLGNT attaches
> **editorial sigla** (⸀ ⸂ ⸃) to tokens. **Count by lemma, not by shape.**
> Lemma counts: **αἰών 122 · αἰώνιος 69 · γενεά 43 · κόσμος 185 ·
> συντέλεια 6 · παρουσία 24 · χρόνος 53 · καιρός 85.**

⚠️ The adjective **αἰώνιος** — 69 occurrences, usually rendered *eternal* or
*everlasting* — is built on this same noun. Whether it means *eternal* or
*pertaining to the age / age-lasting* is a live question and is **not settled
here**; it is flagged because a reader who accepts that αἰών means *age* will
immediately want to ask it.

### That αἰών and κόσμος are not synonyms — from the corpus

**Six verses** in the NT contain both nouns. Two of them settle the question:

- ⭐ **Ephesians 2:2** — `κατὰ τὸν αἰῶνα τοῦ κόσμου τούτου` — *"according to the
  **aiōn of the kosmos** of this."* One is governed by the other. If they meant
  the same thing the phrase would be incoherent.
- ⭐ **Hebrews 9:26** — `ἀπὸ καταβολῆς **κόσμου** … ἐπὶ συντελείᾳ τῶν **αἰώνων**`
  — *"from the founding of the world … at the completion of the ages."* Both
  words, one sentence, different jobs.

### αἰών comes in twos — which "world" cannot do

The corpus uses αἰών in a consistent paired structure: **this one** and **the
coming one**. That pattern only works for an *age*:

- **Matt 12:32** — οὔτε ἐν τούτῳ τῷ αἰῶνι … *nor in this age* (and the verse
  goes on to the coming one)
- **Eph 1:21** — ἐν τῷ αἰῶνι τούτῳ ἀλλὰ καὶ ἐν τῷ μέλλοντι — *in this age but
  also in the coming one*
- **Luke 20:34–35** — οἱ υἱοὶ τοῦ αἰῶνος **τούτου** … τοῦ αἰῶνος **ἐκείνου** —
  *the sons of this age … that age*
- **Mark 10:30** — ἐν τῷ καιρῷ τούτῳ … καὶ ἐν τῷ αἰῶνι τῷ ἐρχομένῳ — *in this
  time … and in the coming age*
- **Heb 6:5** — δυνάμεις τε μέλλοντος αἰῶνος — *powers of the coming age*

There is one κόσμος. There are two αἰῶνες, and the texts move between them.

### ⭐⭐ συντέλεια — all six occurrences, and what every one is bound to

The noun is rare, and the pattern is total:

| | | bound to αἰών? |
|---|---|---|
| **Matt 13:39** | συντέλεια **αἰῶνός** — ⚠️ *anarthrous*, unlike the rest | ✅ |
| **Matt 13:40** | ἐν τῇ συντελείᾳ τοῦ αἰῶνος | ✅ |
| **Matt 13:49** | ἐν τῇ συντελείᾳ τοῦ αἰῶνος | ✅ |
| **Matt 24:3** | συντελείας τοῦ αἰῶνος — the question above | ✅ |
| ⭐ **Matt 28:20** | ἕως τῆς συντελείας τοῦ αἰῶνος | ✅ |
| ⭐ **Heb 9:26** | ἐπὶ συντελείᾳ τῶν αἰώνων | ✅ |

🛑 **Every occurrence of the noun is a completion of an AGE. There is no such
phrase as συντέλεια τοῦ κόσμου anywhere in the New Testament.** Heb 9:26 does
contain κόσμος, but in the other half of the sentence and doing the other job —
*ἀπὸ καταβολῆς **κόσμου*** (from the founding of the world) against *ἐπὶ
συντελείᾳ τῶν **αἰώνων*** (at the completion of the ages). The one verse where
both words appear keeps them apart.

### The verb συντελέω — six more, and none of them cosmic

The related verb occurs six times, and its plainest uses show the word has no
apocalyptic weight of its own:

- **Luke 4:2** — συντελεσθεισῶν, of the **forty days** being completed
- **Acts 21:27** — συντελεῖσθαι, of the **seven days** being completed
- Mark 13:4 · Luke 4:13 · Rom 9:28 · Heb 8:8

⚠️ An earlier draft of this chapter counted eight "occurrences of συντέλεια" by
folding Mark 13:4 and Acts 21:27 — which are the **verb** — into the noun's
list. Noun and verb are counted separately here.

Two of the noun's occurrences are controls that translation practice quietly
concedes:

⭐ **Matthew 28:20** — *"I am with you all the days, ἕως τῆς συντελείας τοῦ
αἰῶνος."* The **identical phrase** as 24:3. No translation renders this one as
a promise that expires when the world is destroyed; it is read as *to the end
of the age*, and understood as continuous.

⭐ **Hebrews 9:26** — *"now once, ἐπὶ συντελείᾳ τῶν αἰώνων, he has been
manifested."* Written in the first century, and it places the **completion of
the ages in its author's own present** — νυνὶ δέ, *but now*. Whatever
συντέλεια names, this writer thought it had arrived.

⭐ **Acts 21:27** is the plainest of all: the verb is used of **seven days**
being completed. The word carries no cosmic weight of its own.

## Part III — what the texts do about *when*

The texts do two things, and they are in tension. Both are in stable
manuscripts, and this project is not going to resolve them for the reader.

### 1. Where a horizon is given, it is near

All three synoptics, near-identically worded:

```
Matt 24:34  ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν ὅτι οὐ μὴ παρέλθῃ ἡ γενεὰ αὕτη ἕως ἂν πάντα ταῦτα γένηται
Mark 13:30  ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν ὅτι οὐ μὴ παρέλθῃ ἡ γενεὰ αὕτη μέχρις οὗ ταῦτα πάντα γένηται
Luke 21:32  ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν ὅτι οὐ μὴ παρέλθῃ ἡ γενεὰ αὕτη ἕως ἂν πάντα γένηται
```

**οὐ μὴ παρέλθῃ** · double negative οὐ μή + aorist subjunctive — the strongest
negation Greek has. *will certainly not pass away*

**ἡ γενεὰ αὕτη** · article + noun, nom. sing. fem., γενεά + demonstrative.
> **43 occurrences in the NT** (MorphGNT lemma count — see the counting note above).
> The corpus shows three distinct uses, and the form here selects none of them:
> 1. **A generation as a countable link in a genealogy** — Matt 1:17,
>    γενεαὶ δεκατέσσαρες, *fourteen generations.*
> 2. **The people alive at one time, addressed** — Matt 11:16, 12:39,
>    τὴν γενεὰν ταύτην, *this generation*, spoken to the crowd in front of him.
> 3. **A kind or sort of people, morally characterised** — Acts 2:40 and
>    Phil 2:15, γενεᾶς σκολιᾶς, *a crooked generation* — closer to *breed* than
>    to a span of years.
>
> Range: *generation · the people of one time · lineage, descent · kind, breed
> · an age or period.* **All three are live here and none is preferred.**

⭐ **The nearest control is one chapter earlier.** **Matthew 23:36** —
`ἥξει ταῦτα πάντα ἐπὶ τὴν γενεὰν ταύτην`, *"all these things will come upon
this generation"* — the same phrase, the same author, thirty verses before, and
there it unambiguously means the people he is speaking to. That does not
*prove* 24:34 means the same, but it is the closest usage there is, and an
honest reading has to weigh it.

### 2. Where a date is asked for, it is refused

```
Matt 24:36  Περὶ δὲ τῆς ἡμέρας ἐκείνης καὶ ὥρας οὐδεὶς οἶδεν
            οὐδὲ οἱ ἄγγελοι τῶν οὐρανῶν οὐδὲ ὁ υἱός εἰ μὴ ὁ πατὴρ μόνος
Mark 13:32  Περὶ δὲ τῆς ἡμέρας ἐκείνης ἢ τῆς ὥρας οὐδεὶς οἶδεν
            οὐδὲ οἱ ἄγγελοι ἐν οὐρανῷ οὐδὲ ὁ υἱός εἰ μὴ ὁ πατήρ
```

**οὐδεὶς οἶδεν** · *no one knows* — οἶδα, perfect with present force, *to know*
by having seen.

⭐ **οὐδὲ ὁ υἱός** · *nor the Son.* Present in both. This is the sort of reading
later copyists found uncomfortable, which counts in its favour rather than
against it.

**Acts 1:7** doubles the refusal, and the two nouns are not synonyms:

> `Οὐχ ὑμῶν ἐστιν γνῶναι **χρόνους** ἢ **καιρούς** οὓς ὁ πατὴρ ἔθετο ἐν τῇ ἰδίᾳ
> ἐξουσίᾳ`

**χρόνος** — *duration, elapsed span, how long.*
**καιρός** — *the appointed or opportune moment, the right time, season.*

> *"It is not yours to know durations or appointed times, which the Father set
> in his own authority."*

The same pair recurs at **1 Thess 5:1** — περὶ δὲ τῶν χρόνων καὶ τῶν καιρῶν.
Both kinds of temporal knowledge are named, and both are declined. He is not
withholding a date; he is declining the whole category.

## What this chapter claims

1. **אַחֲרִית** is an ordinary positional noun — the after-part of a year, a
   sea, a life, a set of days. Its thirteen "latter days" occurrences are
   grammatically identical to Deut 11:12's *end of the year*, and the same noun
   is rendered *a future* at Jer 29:11.
2. **συντέλεια τοῦ αἰῶνος** is *the completion of the age.* **κόσμος does not
   appear in Matthew 24:3.** αἰών and κόσμος co-occur nine times and are governed
   one by the other at Eph 2:2, so they are not interchangeable; αἰών comes in a
   *this one / the coming one* pair that a single κόσμος cannot.
3. **The texts nowhere give a date.** Where they give a horizon it is near
   (γενεὰ αὕτη, thrice); where a date is requested it is refused in the strongest
   available terms, including of the Son.
4. **None of this rests on a manuscript variant.** Every verse above reads the
   same in every witness on disk. The drift is entirely in the English.

## What this chapter does not claim

- That no occurrence of בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים is eschatological. Several sit in
  far-horizon contexts. **Both readings stand; neither is preferred.**
- That γενεά in Matt 24:34 means contemporaries. It carries at least three
  senses, all attested, and the grammar selects none. **All are printed.**
- That "the completion of the age" is a smaller claim than "the end of the
  world." It may be a larger one. The point is only that it is a *different*
  claim, and it is the one the Greek makes.
- Any position on when, whether, or what. The texts decline to say, and this
  chapter declines on the same grounds.

> `tools/.corpus_index.json`. **Greek parse codes and all Greek counts come from

> Greek equivalent of morphhb. An earlier draft carried parse codes written from

> machine-sourced rather than asserted. `gloss.py` was
> not consulted — see `PROVENANCE.md`. Where a semantic range is given for a
> Greek word it is demonstrated from **usage in this corpus**, with verses
> cited, rather than asserted from a lexicon.
