# The Star of Your God / The Synagogue of Satan

**A study of four passages:** Amos 5:26 · Acts 7:43 · Revelation 2:9 · Revelation 3:9

---

## About this document

This merges three working files into one. It is a study of what four biblical
passages actually say in Hebrew and Greek, and — because the question is
unavoidable once you read them — an assessment of the popular claim that the
"star" in Amos is the six-pointed star.

**Short answer on that claim: the evidence does not support it.** The long answer
is Part II.

### How the sources were handled

English quotations are **KJV** (public domain). Where this document gives an
English sense of a Hebrew or Greek word, that is a **gloss of the original**, not
a quotation of any translation.

The originals were read directly from primary texts, not from commentaries:

| Layer | Source |
|---|---|
| Hebrew OT | WLC + OSHB morphology — all 39 books, 23,213 verses |
| Greek NT | SBLGNT text + morphology |
| Septuagint | Rahlfs 1935 and Swete 1887–94 |
| NT manuscripts | CNTR transcriptions — Sinaiticus, Alexandrinus, Vaticanus, Ephraemi, Bezae |

Cross-references were found by **lemma co-occurrence scan** — a celestial lemma
(star, sun, moon, constellations, Mazzaroth, queen-of-heaven, Sakkuth, Kaiwan, or
"host of heaven") appearing together with a cult lemma (bow/worship, serve, burn
incense, libation, sacrifice) — then a second targeted pass. Deuteronomy 4:19 was
used as a control verse to confirm the method worked before any output was trusted.

**Read Part VI before relying on any of this.** It lists exactly what is verified
against primary texts and what is not.

# PART I — The four passages

## 1. Amos 5:26

> "But ye have borne the tabernacle of your Moloch and Chiun your images, the star of your god, which ye made to yourselves."

**Date and setting.** Amos prophesied in the northern kingdom of Israel around
760–750 BC, during the reign of Jeroboam II — a period of military success and
economic prosperity. The book's core charge is that prosperity coexisted with
judicial corruption, exploitation of the poor, and syncretistic worship. Chapter 5
is a funeral lament over Israel; verses 21–27 are God's rejection of Israel's
festivals and offerings, ending in the sentence of exile.

### The key Hebrew terms

| Hebrew (consonants) | KJV | What it likely is |
|---|---|---|
| *skkwt* (סכות) | "tabernacle" | Probably *Sakkut*, an Akkadian astral deity name (Sakkut/SAG.KUD), associated with Saturn. KJV read it as *sukkat*, "booth/tabernacle." |
| *kywn* (כיון) | "Chiun" | *Kaiwan*, the Akkadian *kayamānu*, "the steady one" — the standard Mesopotamian name for the planet Saturn. |

The Masoretic vowel points on both words appear to be borrowed from *shiqquts*
(שִׁקּוּץ), "detestable thing" — a scribal device also used for names like Molech.
So the pointing is a theological comment, not a phonetic record.

### The verse word by word

Regenerated from WLC + OSHB morphology on disk:

```
וּנְשָׂאתֶם   lemma c/5375   morph HC/Vqq2mp
אֵת          lemma 853      morph HTo
סִכּוּת       lemma 5522     morph HNp        <- PROPER NOUN
מַלְכְּכֶם     lemma 4432     morph HNcmsc/Sp2mp
וְאֵת         lemma c/853    morph HC/To
כִּיּוּן       lemma 3594     morph HNp        <- PROPER NOUN
צַלְמֵיכֶם    lemma 6754     morph HNcmpc/Sp2mp
כּוֹכַב       lemma 3556     morph HNcmsc
אֱלֹהֵיכֶם   lemma 430      morph HNcmpc/Sp2mp   <- PLURAL construct
אֲשֶׁר        lemma 834 a    morph HTr
עֲשִׂיתֶם    lemma 6213 a   morph HVqp2mp
לָכֶם        lemma l        morph HR/Sp2mp
```

**✅ CONFIRMED — Sakkuth and Kaiwan are proper nouns.** The morphology dataset tags
both **HNp**, proper noun, independently of any commentary.

**⚠️ CORRECTION TO KJV — "the star of your god" is plural.** אֱלֹהֵיכֶם is
**HNcmpc**, masculine **plural** construct: "the star of your **gods**." Small in
English, but it changes the verse from one deity to a set.

**⚠️ FLAGGED — "your Moloch" is not settled.** מַלְכְּכֶם carries lemma **4432
(Molech)** but morph **HNcmsc/Sp2mp** — a *common* noun in construct with a 2mp
suffix, i.e. "your **king**." The lexicon and the morphology of the same dataset
disagree on this word. Moloch is a *reading*, not a given; "your king" is what the
grammar tags actually encode.

**⚠️ OPEN — the tense is genuinely ambiguous.** וּנְשָׂאתֶם is **HC/Vqq2mp**, waw +
qal *sequential* perfect. That form normally carries forward as a
consequential/future ("and you shall carry…"), which would attach v.26 to the
sentence of exile in v.27 rather than making it a charge about the wilderness
generation. KJV ("ye have borne") and Acts 7:43 (ἀνελάβετε, aorist) both take the
past reading. Both readings are grammatically available; neither should be declared
the winner.

**✅ CONFIRMED — the verse names a star, and gives no point-count.** The
descriptor is כּוֹכַב, "star" (lemma 3556), standing in apposition to צַלְמֵיכֶם,
"your images." **The text does identify the emblem as a star.** What it does not
supply is a number of points or any further geometry — and point-count is the
feature that distinguishes a hexagram from the eight-pointed rosette or radiate
forms. Both statements are established on an exhaustive word list of the verse.

### "The star of your god"

This is the whole description. No shape, no number of points, no color, no
geometry. The phrase functions as an apposition identifying the images just named:
these idols *are* the astral emblem of a planetary deity. In the ancient Near East,
Saturn and other planetary gods were typically depicted by an eight-pointed rosette
or star, a winged disk, or a simple radiate mark — conventions well attested in
Assyrian and Babylonian boundary stones and seals.

### The sentence

Amos 5:27: "Therefore will I cause you to go into captivity beyond Damascus."
Damascus marks the direction of the Assyrian deportation that came in 722 BC.

## 2. Acts 7:43

> "Yea, ye took up the tabernacle of Moloch, and the star of your god Remphan, figures which ye made to worship them: and I will carry you away beyond Babylon."

**Setting.** Stephen's speech before the Sanhedrin, c. AD 34. It is a rapid survey
of Israel's history structured around a single thesis: God's presence was never
confined to a building, and the people repeatedly rejected the deliverers God sent.
Verses 39–43 cover the golden calf and its consequences. Stephen is executed
immediately after finishing.

**Read Acts 7:42 first.** It is the hinge: **παρέδωκεν αὐτοὺς λατρεύειν τῇ στρατιᾷ
τοῦ οὐρανοῦ** — "handed them over to serve the host of heaven." στρατιὰ τοῦ
οὐρανοῦ is the Greek for צבא השמים. Stephen is not only quoting Amos; he is
speaking Deuteronomy 4:19 / 2 Kings 17:16 vocabulary.

### Two differences from Amos

**1. Remphan / Rephan** (Greek Ῥαιφάν, with manuscript variants Rompha, Rephan,
Romphan). This comes from the Septuagint of Amos 5:26, not from the Hebrew. The
common explanation is a transcription slip in the Greek tradition: Hebrew *kaph*
(כ) misread as *resh* (ר). An older suggestion connects it to a Coptic/Egyptian
name for Saturn. Either way, Rephan and Kaiwan stand in the same slot. **But see
the caveat in Part III — the standard explanation does not fully account for the
word.**

**2. "Beyond Babylon"** instead of Amos's "beyond Damascus." Stephen (or his
source) updates the geography to the later and more famous Babylonian exile, which
better fits an address to a Jerusalem audience.

**Why it matters.** Stephen is quoting a text of judgment against Israel's
ancestors and applying it to his hearers. The rhetorical target is idolatry and
resistance to God's messengers. This is prophetic self-criticism from inside the
tradition — the same genre as Amos.

## 3. Revelation 2:9 — to Smyrna

> "I know thy works, and tribulation, and poverty, (but thou art rich) and I know the blasphemy of them which say they are Jews, and are not, but are the synagogue of Satan."

## 4. Revelation 3:9 — to Philadelphia

> "Behold, I will make them of the synagogue of Satan, which say they are Jews, and are not, but do lie; behold, I will make them to come and worship before thy feet, and to know that I have loved thee."

**Setting.** Two of the seven letters to churches in the Roman province of Asia
(western Turkey), most commonly dated to the mid-90s AD under Domitian, though some
date the book to the late 60s. Smyrna and Philadelphia are the only two of the
seven churches that receive no rebuke.

### Notes on the words

- **"Satan"** here is Greek *Satanas*, from Hebrew *satan*, "adversary/accuser."
  The phrase is best read as "**assembly of the adversary**" — a counter-claim
  about who is really opposing God.
- ***Synagōgē*** simply means "assembly" or "gathering." James 2:2 uses it of a
  Christian meeting. **It is not a technical slur in itself**; the force is in the
  genitive that follows.
- The stated grievance is a dispute over the name "Jew" — a claim about who
  legitimately belongs to Israel. In Philadelphia, the promise is that the
  opponents will eventually acknowledge the church, which presumes a **future
  reconciliation, not annihilation**.

### Interpretive options

These are genuinely debated. The main positions:

**1. Intra-Jewish sectarian polemic** — the dominant scholarly reading. The author
is a Jewish follower of Jesus writing to communities in conflict with local
synagogue authorities, probably over exclusion, denunciation to Roman officials, or
access to the legal exemptions Jews held regarding the imperial cult. The rhetoric
belongs to the same family as the Qumran sect calling other Jews "the congregation
of Belial," or the prophets calling Jerusalem "Sodom" (Isaiah 1:10). Insiders
insulting other insiders.

**2. Gentile pretenders** — a minority view: the "false Jews" are non-Jews claiming
Jewish identity for the legal protections it carried.

**3. A general theological category** — later Christian readings extend the phrase
into a timeless label applying beyond the first-century situation.

*Reception, recorded as fact:* this third reading was the basis of Christian
anti-Jewish polemic over many centuries, and Revelation 2:9 and 3:9 are among the
most frequently cited verses in antisemitic literature. That is a statement about
how the reading has been used, not an argument for or against it.

# PART II — The star and the hexagram: the evidence

The question is whether the astral emblem in Amos 5:26 / Acts 7:43 is the
six-pointed star. Below is what each layer of evidence establishes, with dates and
provenance. Draw your own conclusion.

## A. What the primary texts establish

| Finding | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| כִּיּוּן / Kaiwan = Akkadian *kayamānu*, the standard Mesopotamian name for **Saturn** | WLC + OSHB, lemma 3594, tagged HNp (proper noun) | ✅ Verified |
| סִכּוּת / Sakkuth is a proper noun, an astral deity name (Sakkut / SAG.KUD) | WLC + OSHB, lemma 5522, tagged HNp | ✅ Verified |
| The text condemns an astral emblem belonging to these deities | Amos 5:26 in full | ✅ Verified |
| LXX renders the same slot Ραιφαν; Acts 7:43 quotes the Greek | Rahlfs 1935, Swete 1887–94, SBLGNT | ✅ Verified |
| **The verse names the emblem a star** — כּוֹכַב, in apposition to צַלְמֵיכֶם, "your images" | Amos 5:26, lemma 3556 | ✅ Verified |
| **The verse gives no point-count and no further geometry.** Point-count is what separates a hexagram from a rosette or radiate form. | Exhaustive word list of Amos 5:26 | ✅ Verified |

**So the primary texts establish a Saturn-associated emblem that the text itself
calls a star. They are silent on its point-count.**

## B. What secondary literature says about form — 8th century BC

| Claim | Period | Provenance |
|---|---|---|
| Star emblems in Mesopotamia and the Levant took several attested forms — most commonly the **eight-pointed** star or rosette (Ishtar/Venus), also winged disks, crescents, and radiate marks. All are "stars"; they differ in point-count. | 8th c. BC | 🛑 Secondary. Not checkable against texts held here. |
| Attested on Assyrian and Babylonian boundary stones and seals | 8th c. BC | 🛑 Secondary |
| No six-pointed emblem is identified with Saturn/Kaiwan in this period in the sources consulted | 8th c. BC | 🛑 Secondary — and an argument from absence |

## C. The hexagram itself — sourced

### C1. Earliest attestations

| Attestation | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Seal of Joshua ben Asayahu, found at Sidon, bearing a hexagram.** The name is Yahwistic (theophoric in *-yahu*), i.e. a Yahweh-worshipper's seal. | **7th c. BCE** | Scholem, *Kabbalah*, 362–368; noted in Scholem 1949 |
| Hexagram in synagogue decoration (incl. the Capernaum stone), appearing **alongside a pentagram and a swastika-like motif** | 3rd–4th c. CE | Scholem 1949; Oegema 1996 |
| Hexagram on ancient churches and mosques as well as synagogues | Antiquity onward | Scholem 1949 |
| Long-standing use in India — Hindu (Lakshmi) and Buddhist contexts | Ancient, continuous | Oegema 1996 |

**⭐ The Sidon seal is the single most relevant item for this question**, and it was
absent from the earlier version of this document. It places a hexagram in a
**Levantine, Yahwistic context roughly a century after Amos.** Scholem's own reading
is that such early instances are magical signs or ornament and that the meaning is
not clear; he is explicit that the hexagram was **not a distinctively Jewish symbol
for most of Jewish history**. But its presence in that milieu, that close in time, is
a fact and belongs in the record.

### C2. The Saturn question — examined, and the earlier claim here was overstated

An earlier draft of this document stated that a hexagram–Saturn association is "real
and documented" and called it "the strongest link in the chain." **On checking the
sources, that was too generous. Setting out what the sources actually say:**

| Source | Date | What it actually assigns to Saturn |
|---|---|---|
| **Agrippa, *De occulta philosophia libri tres*, Book II** — the foundational text for Western planetary talismanic magic | **1533** | Saturn's figure is a **3×3 magic square** (numbers 3, 9, 15, 45). Its seal is drawn by tracing that square. **Not a hexagram.** |
| **Golden Dawn hexagram rituals** | **late 19th c. (1888+)** | The hexagram is the **generic planetary figure** — six points plus centre for the seven classical planets. Each planet is traced from a different point; Saturn is one of seven. The hexagram is not Saturn's own sigil. |

So in the two systems that actually define planetary sigils in the Western
tradition, **Saturn's emblem is a 3×3 square (Agrippa), and the hexagram is the
figure of the planets collectively rather than of Saturn specifically (Golden
Dawn).**

**On "the hexagram is the talisman of Saturn in Kabbalistic sources":** this claim
circulates widely online. In the sources checked for this document it appears on
occult-interest and religious-polemic websites, **not in the scholarly literature on
the symbol** — not in Scholem, who studied it for fifty years, and not in Oegema's
monograph. That is not proof it is absent from the primary Kabbalistic material,
which was not searched directly here. It does mean the claim is **currently
unsourced in this document**, and it should not be leaned on until someone produces
the primary text.

### C3. Later Jewish adoption

| Event | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Known as **"Seal of Solomon"** — decorative pattern and magical sign, in Jewish *and* Islamic use | Medieval | Scholem 1949; Oegema 1996 |
| Prague Jewish community granted a flag bearing it | 1354 | Scholem 1949 |
| Adopted as a unifying communal symbol — Scholem's argument is that Jews sought an emblem parallel to the cross and the crescent | 18th–19th c. | Scholem 1949 |
| National symbol via the Zionist movement; onto the flag of Israel | 19th–20th c. | Scholem 1949 |

Scholem's overall position: **the hexagram does not originate within Judaism**, and
its career as *the* Jewish symbol is late.

## D. The chronology, laid out

```
c. 760-750 BC   Amos 5:26 — emblem of Saturn-associated deities condemned.
                Called a "star." No point-count given.
    7th c. BC   Seal of Joshua ben Asayahu (Sidon) — HEXAGRAM, Yahwistic name.
   c. AD 34     Acts 7:43 — same text quoted in Greek. No point-count given.
 3rd-4th c AD   Capernaum stone — hexagram present as ornament, with other motifs.
      1533      Agrippa: Saturn's figure is a 3x3 magic square, NOT a hexagram.
   1888 on      Golden Dawn: hexagram = the SEVEN planets generically, not Saturn.
      1354      Prague flag — hexagram as Jewish communal emblem.
  19th-20th c   Hexagram as national symbol.
       20th c   The Amos-to-hexagram identification appears in print.
```

**The interval between Amos and any Jewish use of the hexagram is roughly 2,000
years.** The interval between Amos and the documented hexagram–Saturn association is
comparable.

## E. What would settle it, and is not held here

- Iconographic evidence placing a **six-pointed** form on Saturn or Kaiwan in the
  8th-century BC Levant. Nothing in this corpus bears on it either way.
- Any **pre-20th-century exegetical tradition** making the Amos→hexagram
  identification. Not searched for here; its presence or absence is **unverified**.
- A primary source connecting the medieval Saturn–hexagram association *backward* to
  Near Eastern astral cult, rather than arising independently in European magical
  literature.

## F. Where the argument turns

The chain requires three joins:

**1. Amos condemns worship of Saturn-associated deities.** ✅ Established on the
primary texts — Kaiwan is *kayamānu*, Saturn; Sakkuth is an astral deity name; both
tagged proper nouns.

**2. The hexagram is Saturn's emblem.** ⚠️ **This is the weak join, and it is weaker
than this document previously stated.** Agrippa (1533) gives Saturn a 3×3 magic
square, not a hexagram. Golden Dawn practice (1888+) uses the hexagram for the seven
planets generically, not for Saturn specifically. No scholarly source consulted here
makes the hexagram Saturn's proper sigil. The claim that Kabbalistic sources do so is
**unsourced in this document.**

**3. Therefore the emblem in Amos is the hexagram.** Requires join 2, and requires
retrojecting whatever join 2 establishes back across roughly 2,300 years.

### What genuinely strengthens the case, and belongs on the record

**The Sidon seal.** A hexagram on the seal of Joshua ben Asayahu, 7th century BCE,
Levant, Yahwistic name. **This is a century or so after Amos, in the right region,
in a Yahweh-worshipping context.** It establishes that the hexagram was present and
in use in that world. It does not establish that it was Saturn's emblem, or that it
is what Amos meant — Scholem reads such instances as magical signs or ornament of
unclear meaning. But anyone claiming the hexagram was simply absent from that milieu
is wrong, and this document said something close to that before the sources were
checked.

### So the disagreement reduces to

Amos names a **star** and gives no point-count. A hexagram is attested in the right
region within about a century. Nothing in the primary sources connects that hexagram
to Saturn, and the Saturn–hexagram association that does exist is documented **two
millennia later and generically.**

**Whether that is enough is a judgment about method, not about the text.** The
sources are laid out above; the reader can weigh them.

## G. Reception

Revelation 2:9 and 3:9, and the Amos→hexagram identification, both circulate widely
in modern polemical literature, including antisemitic material where the function is
to assert that contemporary Jews worship Saturn. This is a fact about the arguments'
**reception and use**, recorded here as reception history. It bears on how a reader
will receive the claim; it is not evidence for or against the claim itself.

# PART III — The Rephan chain, verified against the Greek

Both sides of the chain were put on disk and checked.

## The Greek of Amos 5:26, two independent editions

**Rahlfs 1935** (word range 491674–491692):

> καὶ ἀνελάβετε τὴν σκηνὴν τοῦ Μολοχ καὶ τὸ ἄστρον τοῦ θεοῦ ὑμῶν Ραιφαν, τοὺς τύπους αὐτῶν οὓς ἐποιήσατε ἑαυτοῖς

**Swete 1887–94** (diplomatic, Vaticanus-based), `37.Amos.txt` line 37.5.26:

> καὶ ἀνελάβετε τὴν σκηνὴν τοῦ Μόλοχ καὶ τὸ ἄστρον τοῦ θεοῦ ὑμῶν Ῥαιφάν, τοὺς τύπους αὐτῶν οὓς ἐποιήσατε ἑαυτοῖς·

The two editions agree. LXX Amos 5:27 reads **ἐπέκεινα Δαμασκοῦ**.

### ✅ CONFIRMED 1 — Ραιφαν stands exactly where the Hebrew has כִּיּוּן

Regenerated from both texts on disk, not from commentary. The substitution is real.

### ✅ CONFIRMED 2 — Acts 7:43 quotes the Greek, not the Hebrew

| | LXX Amos 5:26–27 | Acts 7:43 (SBLGNT) |
|---|---|---|
| verb | καὶ ἀνελάβετε | καὶ ἀνελάβετε |
| | τὴν σκηνὴν τοῦ Μολοχ | τὴν σκηνὴν τοῦ Μολὸχ |
| | καὶ τὸ ἄστρον τοῦ θεοῦ | καὶ τὸ ἄστρον τοῦ θεοῦ |
| the name | ὑμῶν Ραιφαν | Ῥαιφάν *(ὑμῶν varies — see below)* |
| | τοὺς τύπους **αὐτῶν** | τοὺς τύπους |
| | οὓς ἐποιήσατε | οὓς ἐποιήσατε |
| purpose | **ἑαυτοῖς** "for yourselves" | **προσκυνεῖν αὐτοῖς** "to worship them" |
| sentence | καὶ μετοικιῶ ὑμᾶς ἐπέκεινα | καὶ μετοικιῶ ὑμᾶς ἐπέκεινα |
| destination | **Δαμασκοῦ** | **Βαβυλῶνος** |

The rare form **μετοικιῶ** is shared verbatim. This is quotation of the Greek, full
stop. The Damascus→Babylon substitution is confirmed against the Greek *Vorlage* as
well as the Hebrew — **Stephen is not following a different Hebrew text; he is
changing the Greek he is quoting.**

## ⭐ The manuscripts, read directly

CNTR transcriptions, `class 1/`:

| GA | Manuscript | Date | Reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| **01** | Sinaiticus | c. 350 | του θυ **υμων ρομφαν** |
| **02** | Alexandrinus | 5th c. | του θυ **υμων ραιφαν** |
| **03** | Vaticanus | c. 300–325 | του θυ **ρομφα** *(no υμων)* |
| **04** | Ephraemi | 5th c. | του θυ **υμων ρεφαν** |
| **05** | Bezae | c. 400 | του θυ **ρεμφαμ** *(no υμων)* |

**Five manuscripts, five spellings of the name.**

The SBLGNT apparatus records four — `Ῥαιφάν NA28 ] Ῥομφά WH; Ῥεφάν Treg; Ῥεμφάν RP`
— which is four modern editors each following a different manuscript. **An apparatus
of editions is not an apparatus of manuscripts.** (Note RP's Ῥεμφάν is not even
exactly Bezae's ρεμφαμ, which ends in mu.)

**On ὑμῶν:** 01, 02 and 04 have it and therefore match the LXX exactly; 03 and 05
lack it. SBLGNT prints without it because it follows WH/Treg — i.e. Vaticanus and
Bezae. This **corrects an earlier note** that Acts "drops ὑμῶν": that was one
edition's choice read as the text's.

**⭐ Variant the apparatus does not carry:** Bezae reads **ἐπὶ τὰ μέρη Βαβυλῶνος** —
"to the *regions* of Babylon" — where every other witness has ἐπέκεινα, "beyond."

## 🛑 STILL NOT VERIFIED — the standard Rephan explanation does not reach its own conclusion

The common explanation is a scribal slip: Hebrew *kaph* (כ) misread as *resh* (ר),
yielding *Rywn* → *Raiphan*. Lay the consonants out:

```
Hebrew   כיון      =  K - Y - W - N
Greek    Ραιφαν    =  R - AI - PH - A - N
```

כ→ר accounts for the **R**. **Nothing in the stated explanation accounts for the
φ**, which requires a second substitution (ו→פ). The explanation presents one
confusion where the word needs at least two.

That is not a disproof — multi-step corruption in a name is ordinary — but the
explanation as usually written **does not arrive at the word it is explaining, and
no manuscript on disk shows an intermediate stage.** It is an inference about a lost
stage and should be labelled as one.

Also still open:

- **No LXX apparatus is on disk.** The Rahlfs directory is a single edition with no
  variant record, and Swete is essentially Vaticanus — so for the Greek side of
  Amos this is closer to **one witness than two**. Whether Greek Amos itself varied
  in the name is unchecked. The Göttingen Septuagint, which would settle it, is
  copyrighted.
- **The Coptic/Egyptian Saturn-name alternative** mentioned above: nothing on disk
  bears on it. Unverified.

> **Provenance note.** Rahlfs 1935 and Swete 1887–94 are both **EDITED** texts, and
> both fall past the after-1820 line used in this project. The CNTR transcriptions
> above are the **ORIGINAL** manuscript layer and are what check them — which is why
> the five-spelling table, not the two-edition agreement, is the load-bearing
> evidence here.

# PART V — Reference list for your own Bible

Plain citations only. Every one was confirmed present in the Hebrew or Greek on disk.

## Where the Bible condemns star / sun / moon / host-of-heaven worship

### The law
- **Deuteronomy 4:19** — the base prohibition. Sun, moon, stars = "all the host of heaven." Ends with the clause that God *allotted* them to all the peoples.
- **Deuteronomy 17:2–5** — makes it a capital offense. Penalty is stoning.

### The history books
- **2 Kings 17:16** — the northern kingdom's charge. Closest parallel to Amos.
- **2 Kings 21:3** — Manasseh rebuilds the high places, bows to all the host of heaven.
- **2 Kings 21:5** — builds altars to the host of heaven **inside the two courts of the temple**.
- **2 Kings 23:4** — Josiah removes the vessels made for the host of heaven and burns them.
- **2 Kings 23:5** — the fullest list anywhere: Baal, sun, moon, **the constellations/zodiac**, and all the host of heaven.
- **2 Kings 23:11–12** — **horses dedicated to the sun** removed, **chariots of the sun** burned, rooftop altars torn down.
- **2 Chronicles 33:3** — Chronicles' parallel to 2 Kings 21:3.
- **2 Chronicles 33:5** — parallel to 2 Kings 21:5.

### The prophets
- **Amos 5:26** — the passage under study.
- **Isaiah 47:13** — against Babylon. Astrologers, star-gazers, monthly forecasters, named outright and told to stand up and save her.
- **Jeremiah 7:18** — **the queen of heaven**. Children gather wood, fathers light fire, women knead cakes.
- **Jeremiah 8:2** — sun, moon, all the host of heaven. Five verbs: loved, served, walked after, sought, bowed down to.
- **Jeremiah 10:2** — "do not learn the way of the nations, and do not be dismayed at **the signs of the heavens**."
- **Jeremiah 19:13** — rooftop incense to all the host of heaven.
- **Jeremiah 44:17–19** — the queen of heaven again, and the **only place in the Bible where the people argue back and defend the practice**. They say they had plenty while they did it, and disaster came when they stopped.
- **Jeremiah 44:25** — God's reply to that argument.
- **Ezekiel 8:16** — about 25 men in the inner court of the temple, **backs to the temple, faces east, bowing to the sun**.
- **Zephaniah 1:5** — those who bow on the rooftops to the host of heaven.

### Wisdom
- **Job 31:26–28** — the subtlest one in the Bible. Seeing the moon, the heart **secretly enticed**, the hand kissed to the mouth. No altar, no image, no words — just a gesture. v. 28 still calls it a punishable iniquity and a denial of God.

### New Testament
- **Acts 7:42** — *read this one before 7:43.* "God turned and handed them over to serve **the host of heaven**." This is the hinge verse, and it's Deuteronomy 4:19 vocabulary.
- **Acts 7:43** — Stephen's quotation of Amos 5:26.

## The same words used in the opposite direction

Read these against the list above. The Bible uses the identical vocabulary for the
opposite point, sometimes only a few verses away.

- **Amos 5:8** — *three verses before 5:26*: God as the maker of Pleiades and Orion.
- **Nehemiah 9:6** — "**and the host of heaven bow down to you**." The same verb that is the crime everywhere else.
- **Genesis 2:1** — heaven and earth finished, "and all their host."
- **Psalm 33:6** — the host of heaven made by the breath of his mouth.
- **Isaiah 45:12** — "I commanded all their host."
- **Jeremiah 33:22** — the host of heaven as uncountable.
- **Isaiah 34:4** — all the host of heaven rots and rolls up like a scroll.
- **1 Kings 22:19** and **2 Chronicles 18:18** — the host of heaven standing at God's right and left. This is the divine council, not the pagan sky. (Keep reading: 1 Kings 22:20–22.)
- **Daniel 8:10** — the horn grows up to the host of heaven and casts down some of the host and of the stars.
- **Isaiah 14:12–13** — "shining one, son of the dawn," and "above the **stars of God** I will raise my throne."
- **Genesis 37:9** — sun, moon and eleven stars bowing to Joseph. The only place celestial bodies bow to a human and it is not counted as sin.

- **Psalm 80:14** in English Bibles = **Psalm 80:15** in Hebrew — minor, listed only so the number doesn't confuse you if you compare.

## "Say they are Jews, and are not"

- **Revelation 3:9** — to Philadelphia.

# PART VI — What is and is not verified

**Read this before relying on anything above.** It is the difference between this
document and the material it argues against.

## Verified against primary texts on disk

- ✅ Sakkuth and Kaiwan are tagged proper nouns (OSHB morphology, independent of commentary).
- ✅ אֱלֹהֵיכֶם in Amos 5:26 is plural — "your **gods**," not "your god."
- ✅ **Amos 5:26 names the emblem a star** (כּוֹכַב, in apposition to צַלְמֵיכֶם, "your images"), **and gives no point-count or further geometry.** Both established on an exhaustive word list of the verse.
- ✅ Ραιφαν stands exactly where the Hebrew has כִּיּוּן, in two independent LXX editions.
- ✅ Acts 7:43 quotes the Greek of Amos, not the Hebrew — the rare form μετοικιῶ is shared verbatim.
- ✅ Damascus→Babylon is Stephen changing the Greek he is quoting, not following a different Hebrew text.
- ✅ Five manuscripts give five spellings of the Rephan name (read directly from CNTR transcriptions).
- ✅ Isaiah 60:14 is the source of the image in Revelation 3:9.
- ✅ Every cross-reference in Parts IV and V was confirmed present in the Hebrew or Greek.

## Flagged as unsettled *within* the data

- ⚠️ **"Your Moloch" vs. "your king"** — lexicon and morphology of the same dataset disagree on מַלְכְּכֶם. Moloch is a reading, not a given.
- ⚠️ **The tense of וּנְשָׂאתֶם is ambiguous.** Sequential perfect normally reads forward ("you shall carry"), which would attach v.26 to the exile sentence rather than to the wilderness generation. KJV and Acts both take the past reading. Both are grammatically available.

## 🛑 NOT verified

- 🛑 **The standard Rephan etymology does not account for the φ.** כ→ר gives the R; nothing in the usual explanation gives the φ, which needs a second substitution. No manuscript on disk shows an intermediate stage. It is an inference about a lost stage.
- 🛑 **No LXX apparatus is on disk.** Rahlfs is a single edition with no variant record; Swete is essentially Vaticanus. For the Greek side of Amos this is closer to **one witness than two**. Whether Greek Amos itself varied in the name is unchecked. The Göttingen Septuagint, which would settle it, is copyrighted.
- 🛑 **The Coptic/Egyptian Saturn-name alternative** for Rephan: nothing on disk bears on it.
- 🛑 **"The hexagram is the talisman of Saturn in Kabbalistic sources."** Widely repeated; **not located in the scholarly literature** (absent from Scholem and Oegema as checked). Primary Kabbalistic material was not searched directly. **Unsourced here.**
- ⚠️ **Part II secondary literature was cited, not read in full.** Attributions were checked; arguments were not.
- 🛑 **All historical and iconographic claims in Part II** — Assyrian rosettes, the Capernaum synagogue stone, the 14th-c. Prague flag, the argument's modern provenance — are **secondary-literature claims**, not checkable against the primary texts.
- 🛑 **Semantic ranges are not validator-gated.** The range validator covers 13 Hebrew roots (22.18% of the OT); **none of the roots central to this study are among them.**
- 🛑 **Greek has no root-queue apparatus at all** in this corpus.

## A methodological note worth carrying forward

Four separate times in this work, an **apparatus of editions was mistaken for an
apparatus of manuscripts**. SBLGNT's four printed variants for Rephan are four
modern editors each following a different manuscript — not four manuscript
traditions. Bezae's **ἐπὶ τὰ μέρη Βαβυλῶνος** is a real variant that no printed
apparatus consulted here carries at all.

**When it matters, read the manuscripts.**

# PART VII — How to work through it

## Reading order

1. **Deuteronomy 4:19** → **Deuteronomy 17:2–5** — the rule and the penalty.
2. **2 Kings 17:16** → **Amos 5:26** — the charge against the northern kingdom.
3. **2 Kings 21:5** → **Ezekiel 8:16** — how far into the temple it got.
4. **2 Kings 23:4–12** — the purge, and the fullest list of what was being worshipped.
5. **Jeremiah 44:17–19** — the people's own defense of it, in their words.
6. **Job 31:26–28** — the version with no altar at all.
7. **Amos 5:8** then **Nehemiah 9:6** — the deliberate reversals.
8. **Acts 7:42–43** — the whole thing picked up and aimed at a live audience.

## Study questions

1. Why does Stephen change "beyond Damascus" to "beyond Babylon"? What does the substitution do for his argument?
2. Amos 5:21–24 rejects worship and demands justice; 5:25–27 charges idolatry. Are these one indictment or two?
3. In Revelation 3:9, the opponents come and worship at the church's feet. What does that outcome imply about what the author thinks of them?
4. Both Amos and Stephen aim their harshest words at their own people. **What changes when a later reader aims the same words at someone else's?**
5. Amos 5:26 calls the emblem a star but gives no point-count. When a text names a category and not its specifics, what settles the specifics — and who carries the burden?
