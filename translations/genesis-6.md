# Genesis 6:1–4

Same method as `OUR-TRANSLATION.md`: translated from the consonantal Hebrew
(`hebrew-ot/morphhb`), using morphological parse codes and Strong's-number root
identification only. No published English translation, ancient or modern, and
no gloss file, was consulted. Where the grammar is genuinely ambiguous, every
reading the grammar permits is given — none is picked silently.

Regenerate the underlying data with:
```
./tools/analyze.py Gen 6 1 --range 4
./tools/allwitness.py Gen 6 1   (and 2, 3, 4)
```

---

# Genesis 6:1

## Word by word

**וַֽיְהִי** · `ו/יהי` · `HC/Vqw3ms` — conjunction + verb, qal, wayyiqtol 3ms
> Root **היה**, "to be, become, happen." The narrative-opening formula.
> **"And it came to pass"**

**כִּי** · `כי` · `HC` — conjunction
> Range: *that · because · when.* Introduces the clause that follows; here
> temporal-causal. **"that / when"**

**הֵחֵל** · `החל` · `HVhp3ms` — verb, hiphil, perfect 3ms
> Root **חלל** (the "begin" homonym, distinct from the "profane/pierce" homonym
> that shares the same Strong's number, 2490 — the tool's data disambiguates
> them as "2490 c"). Hiphil: to cause to begin, to undertake first.
> Range: *to begin · to start · to undertake.* **"began"**

**הָאָדָם** · `ה/אדם` · `HTd/Ncmsa` — article + noun, masc sing absolute
> **אדם**: humankind collectively, or a human being. **Definite here** ("the
> man" / "mankind" as a defined class) — and definite at every occurrence in
> this passage (see note below on the pattern across all four verses).
> **"humankind"**

**לָרֹב** · `ל/רב` · `HR/Vqc` — preposition + verb, qal, infinitive construct
> Root **רבב**, to be or become many/numerous. With לְ + infinitive construct,
> a purpose/result clause. Range: *to multiply · to increase · to become
> numerous.* **"to multiply"**

**עַל־פְּנֵי** · `על/פני` · `HR + HNcbpc` — preposition + noun, construct
> **פנים**, "face," construct. The idiom "upon the face of" = over the surface
> of. **"upon the face of"**

**הָאֲדָמָה** · `ה/אדמה` · `HTd/Ncfsa` — article + noun, fem sing absolute
> **אדמה**: ground, soil, arable land — cultivable earth, distinct from אֶרֶץ
> ("land, territory, earth" more broadly, used later in v.4). **אדם/אדמה** sit
> side by side here as a visual and aural pair; whether the two nouns share an
> etymological root or the resemblance is coincidental is disputed among
> Hebraists, so this is noted as a textual feature (paronomasia), not asserted
> as an established etymology. **"the ground"**

**וּבָנוֹת** · `ו/בנות` · `HC/Ncfpa` — conjunction + noun, fem plur absolute
> **בת**, "daughter." **"and daughters"**

**יֻלְּדוּ** · `ילדו` · `HVPp3cp` — verb, pual, perfect 3cp
> Root **ילד**, "to bear, beget." Pual = passive of the intensive stem.
> Range: *were born.* **"were born"**

**לָהֶם** · `ל/הם` · `HR/Sp3mp` — preposition + suffix 3mp. **"to them"**

## The verse — Masoretic text

> **And it came to pass, when humankind began to multiply upon the face of
> the ground, and daughters were born to them,**

# Genesis 6:2

## Word by word

**וַיִּרְאוּ** · `ו/יראו` · `HC/Vqw3mp` — conjunction + verb, qal, wayyiqtol 3mp
> Root **ראה**, "to see." **"and [they] saw"**

**בְנֵי־הָאֱלֹהִים** · `בני` + `ה/אלהים` · `HNcmpc` + `HTd/Ncmpa`
> **בְּנֵי**: construct plural of **בן**, "son." Construct state — broader than
> biological sonship; elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible "sons of X" regularly means
> members of a class or group (e.g. "sons of the prophets" = members of a
> prophetic guild). Range: *sons of · members of · those belonging to.*
>
> **הָאֱלֹהִים**: **אלהים**, plural in form. Takes singular agreement when used
> of the God of Israel; plural agreement when used of gods or divine beings
> generally. It carries the **definite article here** — this is worth stating
> plainly, because the article does not by itself settle which of the word's
> ranges is meant: הָאֱלֹהִים ("the god/gods") is *also* the ordinary way
> biblical Hebrew narrative refers to the God of Israel (it is how much of
> Genesis 1–3 refers to him), so the article is compatible with a single
> definite God, with a definite class of divine beings ("the gods/divine
> ones," referred to as a known category), or — a rarer, but real, attested
> range of אלהים elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible (of human judges/magistrates,
> e.g. in legal contexts) — with "the rulers/mighty ones," a reading some later
> interpretive traditions have taken.
>
> **What the phrase can denote, without resolving it:**
> 1. Sons of God — members of a heavenly/divine court subordinate to God
> 2. Sons of the gods/divine beings, as a class
> 3. Sons of the rulers/judges (the rarer human-magistrate sense of אלהים)
>
> The consonants and the article permit all three; nothing in the grammar of
> this verse forces one.
>
> **Connection to Deut 32:8:** this project has already established from disk
> (scroll 4Q37 and the Septuagint, against the Masoretic בני ישראל) that
> Deuteronomy 32:8's older reading is **בני אלהים**, "sons of God" — there
> *without* the article. Genesis 6:2's phrase is the same construct + noun,
> here *with* the article, and it is uncontested across the witnesses on
> disk — no variant reading turns up for בני האלהים itself in Gen 6:2/6:4.
> The two passages use kindred vocabulary for a group of divine/semi-divine
> "sons"; that is as far as the shared phrase goes. It does not by itself
> establish that Gen 6 and Deut 32 mean the same beings, only that the same
> construction (בני + אלהים, "sons of God/gods") is attested, uncontested,
> in both places.

**אֶת** · `את` · `HTo` — direct object marker (untranslated)

**בְּנוֹת הָאָדָם** · `בנות` + `ה/אדם` · `HNcfpc` + `HTd/Ncmsa`
> Construct **בְּנוֹת** ("daughters of") + definite **הָאָדָם** ("humankind" —
> definite again, as in v.1). **"the daughters of humankind"**

**כִּי** · `כי` · `HC` — conjunction, here complementizer after a verb of
perception. **"that"**

**טֹבֹת** · `טבת` · `HAafpa` — adjective, fem plur absolute
> Root **טוב**. Broad range: *good · pleasing · fair · agreeable* — the
> adjective covers moral, aesthetic, and practical goodness alike; nothing in
> the form narrows it to one sense over another. **"good / fair"**

**הֵנָּה** · `הנה` · `HPp3fp` — independent pronoun, 3fp. **"they [were]"**

**וַיִּקְחוּ** · `ו/יקחו` · `HC/Vqw3mp` — conjunction + verb, qal, wayyiqtol 3mp
> Root **לקח**, "to take, take hold of, receive." With a woman as object,
> this verb regularly carries the extended sense "to take in marriage."
> **"and [they] took"**

**לָהֶם נָשִׁים** · `ל/הם` + `נשים` · `HR/Sp3mp` + `HNcfpa`
> **נָשִׁים**, plural of אִשָּׁה ("woman"), suppletive form. **"for themselves
> wives / women"**

**מִכֹּל אֲשֶׁר בָּחָרוּ** · `מ/כל` + `אשר` + `בחרו`
> **מִכֹּל**: "from all/any." **אֲשֶׁר**: relative particle, "that/whom."
> **בָּחָרוּ**: root **בחר**, "to choose, select, prefer" — qal perfect 3cp.
> **"from all/whomever they chose"**

## The verse — Masoretic text

> **the sons of God saw the daughters of humankind, that they were good, and
> they took for themselves wives from all/whomever they chose.**

## The Septuagint — a real Greek variant at exactly this word

`./tools/allwitness.py Gen 6 2` returns the **Rahlfs** critical text:

> `ἰδόντες δὲ οἱ **υἱοὶ** τοῦ θεοῦ τὰς θυγατέρας τῶν ἀνθρώπων...`
> ("the **sons** of God")

But Rahlfs is an eclectic edition. Checked separately against
`septuagint/LXX-Swete` — Swete's edition, which prints **Codex Vaticanus** as
its base text (`PROVENANCE.md`: "Vaticanus-based") — both the raw transcription
(`src/First1KGreek-LXX-RAW/1.Genesis.txt`, line 138) and the annotated edition
(`src/First1KGreek-LXX-XML/tlg001/tlg0527.tlg001.opp-grc2.xml`, verse div n="2")
read:

> `ἰδόντες δὲ οἱ **ἄγγελοι** τοῦ ... τὰς θυγατέρας τῶν ἀνθρώπων...`
> ("the **angels/messengers**")

**Two different Greek words at the same spot, in two editions on disk.** Note
also: this transcription has a gap — the word after `τοῦ` (grammatically a
genitive noun, expected `θεοῦ`) is not present in the file; `τοῦ` runs directly
into `τὰς`. This may be a real absence in Vaticanus at this point or a
digitization loss (the same raw file drops whitespace between other adjacent
words elsewhere in the chapter, e.g. `τὸ φῶςκαὶ`, `ἦσανὁ θεός`), and this file
alone does not let me tell which. What is *not* in doubt is the headword
itself: ἄγγελοι, not υἱοί.

Swete's own critical apparatus for this chapter (same XML file, footnote block)
adds a manuscript-level data point:

> `2 ἄγγελοι sup ras Α ?vid`

Meaning: at verse 2, the word **ἄγγελοι** in **Codex Alexandrinus (A)** is
written **"sup ras"** — over an erasure — with the reading itself marked
uncertain (`?vid`, *ut videtur*, "as it appears"). Something at this exact
word was physically altered in Alexandrinus; what stood there before the
correction is not recoverable from this note. This is the same category of
evidence as the ΟΣ→ΘΣ correction already documented in `HIGHLIGHTS.md` for
1 Timothy 3:16 — a manuscript recording, in its own physical condition, that a
scribe or corrector touched this specific word.

By contrast, **verse 4's** בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים is translated **υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ**
("sons of God") in *both* Rahlfs and Swete, with no variant flagged — the two
editions agree there. The instability is localized to v.2.

One further, separate observation from the same Greek: at v.4 the LXX renders
**both** הַנְּפִלִים and (later in the same verse) הַגִּבֹּרִים with the *same*
Greek word, γίγαντες — collapsing a distinction the Hebrew makes between two
different terms. See v.4 below.

**What this does and doesn't show:** the Greek tradition itself is divided
between "angels" and "sons [of God]" for בני האלהים at Gen 6:2, and at least
one major manuscript (Alexandrinus) shows physical evidence of alteration at
that word. This is evidence that ancient readers disagreed about, or edited,
how to render the phrase — not evidence for what the underlying Hebrew phrase
itself denotes, which (per above) the Hebrew grammar alone does not settle.

# Genesis 6:3

## Word by word

**וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה** · `ו/יאמר` + `יהוה` — "and YHWH said." Root **אמר**, "to
say." The divine name left untransliterated, as elsewhere in this project.

**לֹא** · `לא` · `HTn` — negative particle. **"not"**

**יָדוֹן** · `ידון` · `HVqi3ms` — verb, qal, imperfect 3ms
> **The most difficult word in the passage. Root genuinely uncertain.** The
> parse code confirms only the *form* — qal imperfect 3ms — not which root it
> belongs to. The Masoretic vocalization (the hiraq/holam pointing) already
> reflects one interpretive choice by the Masoretes; the bare consonants ידון
> do not by themselves decide between candidates. Three roots are proposed:
>
> 1. **דון / דין** — "to judge, rule, contend, plead a cause, strive at law."
>    (Strong's number attached to this word, 1777, groups the two spellings
>    דּוּן/דִּין as variant forms of one root under this sense.) On this root:
>    **"my spirit shall not strive / contend / act as judge in humankind
>    forever."**
> 2. **דנן**, connected by some to a sense of remaining, abiding, or being
>    overpowered/humbled — a root not otherwise well-attested in Biblical
>    Hebrew, proposed partly because the Septuagint here renders with
>    καταμείνῃ, "remain, stay" (see below), which fits an "abide" sense better
>    than "judge/strive" does. **"my spirit shall not remain / abide in
>    humankind forever."**
> 3. **נדן** — "sheath" (of a sword; cf. the noun נָדָן). A minority proposal
>    connects יָדוֹן to this noun, on the image of the body as a sheath
>    "housing" the spirit: **"my spirit shall not be sheathed [i.e. housed] in
>    humankind forever."**
>
> All three are attempts to explain the same four consonants; the grammar of
> this verse does not adjudicate between them, and the Septuagint's rendering
> (below) is itself only evidence of how one ancient translator resolved the
> difficulty, not proof of which Hebrew root stood behind it.

**רוּחִי** · `רוח/י` · `HNcbsc/Sp1cs` — noun + suffix 1cs
> **רוח**: broad range — *wind · breath · spirit.* Construct + 1cs suffix.
> **"my spirit / my breath"**

**בָאָדָם** · `ב/אדם` · `HRd/Ncmsa` — preposition (with assimilated article) +
noun
> The parse marks the preposition itself as carrying the article (`Rd`) — so
> this is **definite**: "in *the* man / humankind," continuing the pattern of
> every other occurrence of אדם in this passage. **"in humankind"**

**לְעֹלָם** · `ל/עלם` · `HR/Ncmsa` — preposition + noun
> **עולם**: a noun of indefinite temporal duration — an age, a long or remote
> stretch of time — with no inherent direction of its own. Direction is
> supplied by the preposition: לְ + עולם looks forward ("into an age," i.e.
> perpetually, forever); מִן + עולם (v.4 below) looks backward ("from an
> age," i.e. long ago, of old). **"forever"**

**בְּשַׁגַּם** · `ב/ש/גם` · `HR/Tr/D` — preposition + relative particle + adverb
> **Contested parsing.** This project's morphological data breaks the word
> into three morphemes: בְּ ("in, because"), the short relative שֶׁ ("that,"
> a form of אֲשֶׁר), and גַּם ("also, even"), giving a causal sense: "inasmuch
> as also / since indeed." That decomposition is grammatically live but not
> the only one on record — some treat שׁ + גם as an inseparable idiom
> functioning as a single causal conjunction, and a minority view relates the
> spelling to a root שגה/שגג ("to err, go astray," the noun שְׁגָגָה "error"),
> yielding a sense closer to "in their erring," though the parse data here
> does not analyze the word that way. **Given all this, translated as: "since
> / inasmuch as also."**

**הוּא בָשָׂר** · `הוא` + `בשר` — pronoun 3ms + noun, masc sing absolute
> **בשר**: flesh, physical body. **"he [is] flesh"**

**וְהָיוּ יָמָיו** · `ו/היו` + `ימי/ו` — conjunction + verb (qal, weqatal 3cp)
+ noun/suffix
> **היה**, "to be"; **ימים** ("days") + 3ms suffix. **"and his days shall be"**

**מֵאָה וְעֶשְׂרִים שָׁנָה** · numeral + numeral + noun. **"a hundred and
twenty years"**

## The verse — Masoretic text

> **And YHWH said, "My spirit shall not [contend/strive · or: remain/abide ·
> or: be sheathed] in humankind forever, since he too is flesh; and his days
> shall be a hundred and twenty years."**

## The Septuagint, for comparison of how the difficulty was resolved

`./tools/allwitness.py Gen 6 3` gives Rahlfs:
> `οὐ μὴ καταμείνῃ τὸ πνεῦμά μου ἐν τοῖς ἀνθρώποις τούτοις εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα...`

**καταμείνῃ** = "remain, stay behind, abide." The Greek translator resolved
יָדוֹן toward the "abide/remain" sense (candidate 2 above), not toward
"judge/strive" (candidate 1) or "sheathed" (candidate 3). This is one ancient
translator's judgment call on a word the Hebrew itself leaves ambiguous — it
narrows the live candidates in the history of interpretation, but it does not
settle the Hebrew root.

# Genesis 6:4

## Word by word

**הַנְּפִלִים** · `ה/נפלים` · `HTd/Ncmpa` — article + noun, masc plur absolute
> **The Nephilim. Etymology genuinely disputed — stated plainly, not
> resolved.** The form is often associated with root **נפל**, "to fall" — but
> the derivational pattern from נפל to a noun נְפִיל is not a straightforward,
> regular one, which is exactly why the connection is disputed rather than
> settled. If the נפל connection holds, the sense would run toward "fallen
> ones" (fallen from heaven, or those who fall/attack). If it does not, the
> word may be a loanword or an otherwise unattested term whose original sense
> is simply not recoverable from Hebrew alone. **Both possibilities are live;
> neither is asserted here.**
>
> **The only other occurrence in the Hebrew Bible is Num 13:33** — `הַנְּפִילִים`
> (plene spelling there, defective here; same root/word). There the term
> appears in apposition with "sons of Anaq" (בְּנֵי עֲנָק), a group elsewhere
> associated with great physical stature; the text explicitly says "we were
> in our own eyes like grasshoppers" (כַּחֲגָבִים) beside them. That verse
> supplies context for how the word was understood by a later biblical writer,
> not a definition of the word's origin.
>
> **The Septuagint here renders הַנְּפִלִים as γίγαντες**, "giants" —
> reflecting how the Greek translator understood the term's referent (huge
> beings), which is a datum about ancient reception, not a recovery of the
> Hebrew etymology.

**הָיוּ בָאָרֶץ** · `היו` + `ב/ארץ` · `HVqp3cp` + `HRd/Ncbsa`
> **היה**, qal perfect 3cp, "were, existed." **ארץ**, "land/earth," definite
> (article assimilated into the preposition, as with אדם in v.3).
> **"were in the land"**

**בַּיָּמִים הָהֵם** · `ב/ימים` + `ה/הם` · `HRd/Ncmpa` + `HTd/Pp3mp`
> "In the days" + article + independent pronoun 3mp used demonstratively
> ("those/them" → attributive "those"), the regular Hebrew pattern for a
> demonstrative modifying a definite noun. **"in those days"**

**וְגַם אַחֲרֵי־כֵן** · `ו/גם` + `אחרי` + `כן`
> **גַּם**: "also, even" (same particle discussed under v.3's בשגם).
> **אַחֲרֵי כֵן**: "after so/thus" — a set idiom, "afterward." **"and also
> afterward"**

**אֲשֶׁר יָבֹאוּ בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים אֶל בְּנוֹת הָאָדָם** · `אשר` + `יבאו` (`HVqi3mp`,
qal imperfect, root **בוא**) + `בני האלהים` (as in v.2) + `אל` (preposition,
"to/unto") + `בנות האדם` (as in v.2)
> **בוא**: root range *to come · to go · to enter.* בוא + אל directed at a
> person is a construction this same corpus of narrative Hebrew elsewhere uses
> for a man's coming-to a woman in the specific sense of sexual union, as well
> as its plain literal sense of physical approach/entry; context, not the verb
> alone, decides which is meant. Both are stated as live: **"went in to / came
> to"** the daughters of humankind. **בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים** again carries the same
> range discussed at v.2 — uncontested phrase, contested referent.

**וְיָלְדוּ לָהֶם** · `ו/ילדו` · `HC/Vqq3cp` — conjunction + verb, qal, weqatal 3cp
> Root **ילד**. The **qal** stem of this verb ordinarily denotes the mother's
> act of *bearing* a child (as in v.1's יֻלְּדוּ, pual, "were born"); the
> hiphil stem is the one regularly used for the father's act of *begetting*.
> **The grammatical subject of this 3cp form is ambiguous** — 3rd common
> plural does not distinguish gender in this conjugation, so the form itself
> cannot tell us whether "they" resumes בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים (the nearer
> grammatical subject in the sentence, "begetting" — an unusual use of the
> qal stem for that sense) or refers back to "the daughters of humankind"
> (the more natural subject for a qal form of ילד, "bearing"). Both readings
> are grammatically available; the form does not resolve it. **"and they bore
> [children] to them"**

**הֵמָּה הַגִּבֹּרִים** · `המה` + `ה/גברים` · `HPp3mp` + `HTd/Aampa`
> **הֵמָּה**: independent pronoun 3mp, resumptive/emphatic, "they."
> **גִּבּוֹר**, from root **גבר**, "to be strong, prevail, be mighty." Range:
> *strong one · mighty man · warrior · hero.* **"they [were] the mighty ones"**

**אֲשֶׁר מֵעוֹלָם** · `אשר` + `מ/עולם` · `HR/Ncmsa`
> Relative clause with an elided verbal predicate ("who [were]"), + **מִן +
> עולם** — as noted at v.3, the מִן here directs the same noun of indefinite
> duration *backward*: "from an age," i.e. of old, from ancient times, long
> ago — the mirror of v.3's forward-looking לְעֹלָם. **"who [were] from of
> old"**

**אַנְשֵׁי הַשֵּׁם** · `אנשי` + `ה/שם` · `HNcmpc` + `HTd/Ncmsa`
> **אַנְשֵׁי**: construct plural of **אִישׁ**, "man." **הַשֵּׁם**: "the name" —
> **שֵׁם** ranges beyond a literal personal name to *reputation, renown, fame*
> (a "name" that is known/talked-of). "Men of the name" = **"men of renown /
> men of name [i.e. renowned, well-known men]"**

## The verse — Masoretic text

> **The Nephilim were in the land in those days, and also afterward, when the
> sons of God went in to the daughters of humankind and they bore [children]
> to them — they [were] the mighty ones who [were] from of old, men of
> renown.**

## The Septuagint, and a Greek collapse of two Hebrew terms

`./tools/allwitness.py Gen 6 4` gives Rahlfs (matching Swete here, no variant
flagged):
> `οἱ δὲ **γίγαντες** ἦσαν ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς ... εἰσεπορεύοντο οἱ **υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ**
> πρὸς τὰς θυγατέρας τῶν ἀνθρώπων ... ἐκεῖνοι ἦσαν οἱ **γίγαντες** οἱ ἀπ᾿
> αἰῶνος, οἱ ἄνθρωποι οἱ ὀνομαστοί.`

Two things worth stating plainly:
- **בְּנֵי הָאֱלֹהִים = υἱοὶ τοῦ θεοῦ** here, matching both Greek editions —
  the "angels" variant found at v.2 in Swete does *not* recur at v.4.
- **The Hebrew's two distinct terms — הַנְּפִלִים (v.4a) and הַגִּבֹּרִים
  (v.4b) — are both rendered γίγαντες** ("giants") in the Greek. Whatever
  distinction the Hebrew draws between "the Nephilim" and "the mighty ones"
  is not preserved in this Greek translation; the LXX treats them as the same
  referent, translated once each with the same word. That is evidence about
  how the Greek translator read the passage (as being about one group under
  two names), not evidence that the Hebrew itself treats them as identical.

## Summary of what is genuinely uncertain here, and why

- **בְּנֵי־הָאֱלֹהִים** (vv.2, 4): uncontested in the Hebrew witnesses on disk,
  but the phrase's *referent* is not settled by grammar alone — "sons of God,"
  "sons of the gods/divine beings," and (on the rarer sense of אלהים as human
  rulers) "sons of the judges/mighty men" are all grammatically live. The
  Greek tradition disagrees with itself at v.2 (ἄγγελοι in Swete/Vaticanus vs.
  υἱοί in Rahlfs), with manuscript evidence (Codex Alexandrinus, "sup ras
  ?vid") that the word was physically altered at that spot — but agrees at
  v.4. This mirrors, without proving identical to, the בני אלהים / בני ישראל
  divergence already documented for Deut 32:8 in this project.
- **נְפִלִים** (v.4): root disputed — a connection to נפל ("to fall") is
  proposed but the derivational pattern is irregular, so a loanword or
  otherwise-unrecovered origin is equally live. Etymology is unknown, stated
  as such. Its only other occurrence, Num 13:33, associates it with the
  "sons of Anaq," described there as men of great stature.
- **יָדוֹן** (v.3): root disputed among דון/דין ("judge, strive, contend"),
  a proposed דנן ("abide, remain" — the sense the LXX's καταμείνῃ leans
  toward), and a minority נדן-derived ("sheathe") reading. The consonants and
  parse code confirm only the verb's form (qal imperfect 3ms), not its root.
- **בְּשַׁגַּם** (v.3): the on-disk parse divides it בְּ + relative שֶׁ + גַּם
  ("since/inasmuch as also"), which is grammatically defensible but not the
  only proposal on record; an idiom reading and a שגה/שגג-derived ("erring")
  reading also exist.
- **וְיָלְדוּ לָהֶם** (v.4): the 3cp verb form does not grammatically specify
  whether its subject is "the sons of God" (begetting) or "the daughters of
  humankind" (bearing) — both are permitted by the form.

Nothing above is resolved beyond what the Hebrew grammar and the witnesses on
disk actually show.
