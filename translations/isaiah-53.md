# Isaiah 53:11

Translated from the Hebrew. No English translation was consulted or adapted.
Method: `translations/OUR-TRANSLATION.md`.

This verse carries the strongest witness convergence in this project. **Three
separate Hebrew scrolls and the Greek translation all contain a word the Masoretic
text does not**, and its absence leaves a grammatical gap.

⚠️ Scroll readings required querying book code **`Is`** directly — `allwitness.py`
returns nothing for `Isa`. Same abbreviation mismatch noted in
`translations/isaiah-7-and-14.md`.

---

## Word by word

**מֵעֲמַל** · `מ/עמל` — preposition מִן + **עָמָל**, construct
> **עמל**: *toil · labour · trouble · misery · the fruit of labour*. The word covers
> both the effort and the suffering it costs — English has no single equivalent.
> **"from the toil of" / "out of the anguish of"**

**נַפְשׁוֹ** · `נפש/ו` — **נֶפֶשׁ** + 3ms suffix
> **נפש** is not "soul" in the later philosophical sense. Its concrete base is
> *throat · breath*, extending to *life · living being · appetite · self · person*.
> Range: *his throat · his breath · his life · his very self*.
> **"his life" / "his very self"**
> ⚠️ 1QIsaa spells it **נפשוה**, a fuller orthography. Spelling, not sense.

**יִרְאֶה** · `יראה` — qal imperfect 3ms of **ראה**
> *to see · to look at · to perceive · to experience*. **"he shall see"**
>
> **In the Masoretic text this verb has no object.** "He shall see, he shall be
> satisfied" — see *what*? The gap is real, and every witness below fills it.

**[אוֹר]** · `אור` — **present in three scrolls and the Greek; absent from the Masoretic**
> **אור**: *light · daylight · illumination*. **"light"**

**יִשְׂבָּע** · `ישבע` — qal imperfect 3ms of **שבע**
> *to be sated · to have one's fill · to be satisfied* — the ordinary word for
> eating one's fill. **"he shall be satisfied"**
> ⚠️ 1QIsaa reads **וישבע** (with conjunction); 4Q58 reads **ושבע**.

**בְּדַעְתּוֹ** · `ב/דעת/ו` — preposition + **דַּעַת** + 3ms suffix
> Root **ידע**, *to know*. דעת: *knowledge · perception · discernment*.
> The preposition בְּ is genuinely open here: instrumental **"by his knowledge"**,
> or causal **"because of his knowledge"**. The suffix is also open — knowledge
> *he* possesses, or knowledge *of him*. Hebrew suffixes do not distinguish
> subjective from objective genitive. **Both readings stand.**

**יַצְדִּיק** · `יצדיק` — **hiphil** imperfect 3ms of **צדק**
> Qal צדק is *to be righteous · to be in the right*. The **hiphil is causative or
> declarative**, and the two are not the same: *to make righteous* or *to declare
> righteous* — also, in legal settings, *to acquit · to vindicate*.
> Range: *he shall make righteous · he shall declare righteous · he shall vindicate · he shall acquit*.
> **Genuinely undetermined by the morphology. All are given.**

**צַדִּיק** · `צדיק` — adjective used substantivally
> *righteous · just · in the right*. **"a righteous one"**
> Its syntax here is contested: it can be the object of יַצְדִּיק ("he shall
> vindicate a righteous one"), or stand in apposition to what follows ("the
> righteous one, my servant"). Word order permits both.

**עַבְדִּי** · `עבד/י` — **עֶבֶד** + **1st person** suffix
> **עבד**: *servant · slave · subject · worshipper*. The range runs from chattel
> slavery to a title of honour. **"my servant"**
> ⚠️ **1QIsaa reads עַבְדּוֹ — "HIS servant", 3rd person.** A real variant. 1Q8,
> 4Q56 and 4Q58 all read "my servant" with the Masoretic.

**לָרַבִּים** · `ל/רבים` — preposition + **רַב** plural, determined
> *many · great · numerous*. With the article: **"for the many"**.
> The preposition לְ is wide: *for · to · with respect to · on behalf of*.

**וַעֲוֺנֹתָם** · `ו/עונת/ם` — conjunction + **עָוֺן** plural + 3mp suffix
> **עון**: *iniquity · guilt · the punishment for guilt*. The word runs the whole
> arc — the act, the culpability, and the consequence — and Hebrew does not
> separate them. **"and their iniquities"**

**הוּא** · `הוא` — independent pronoun, emphatic. **"he himself"**
> ⚠️ 1QIsaa reads **הואה**, fuller spelling.

**יִסְבֹּל** · `יסבל` — qal imperfect 3ms of **סבל**
> *to bear · to carry · to bear a load*. A physical porterage word.
> **"shall bear" / "shall carry"**

## The verse — Masoretic text (Leningrad Codex, 1008 CE)

> **Out of the anguish of his life he shall see, he shall be satisfied.
> By his knowledge shall my righteous servant vindicate the many,
> and their iniquities he himself shall bear.**

*(the object of "see" is missing; the Hebrew simply does not supply one)*

## The verse — the scrolls and the Greek

> **Out of the anguish of his life he shall see light, and be satisfied.
> By his knowledge shall my righteous servant vindicate the many,
> and their iniquities he himself shall bear.**

## The witnesses

| Witness | Date | "light"? | servant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1QIsaa** | c. 125 BCE | **יראה אור** yes | **עבדו** — *his* servant |
| **1Q8** | c. 50 BCE–68 CE | **יראה אור** yes | עבדי — my servant |
| **4Q58** | c. 50 BCE | **יראה אור** yes | עבדי — my servant |
| 4Q56 | c. 50 BCE | *(fragmentary — begins at "servant")* | עבדי — my servant |
| **Septuagint** | transl. c. 200–150 BCE | **δεῖξαι αὐτῷ φῶς** — "to show him light" | — |
| **Masoretic** | 1008 CE | **absent** | עבדי — my servant |

## What this shows

**Four independent witnesses carry "light"; the Masoretic manuscript does not.**
Three are Hebrew scrolls, one is a Greek translation made from a Hebrew text —
and they are independent of each other. This is the widest convergence against
the Masoretic text found anywhere in this project, wider than Deuteronomy 32:8
(one scroll plus the Greek) or Psalm 22:16.

It also repairs a defect. In the Masoretic text יִרְאֶה — "he shall see" — has no
object, which is not ordinary Hebrew. The scrolls and the Greek supply one.

Two accounts fit, and the manuscripts do not choose between them:

- **אוֹר dropped out.** The eye skips from יראה to the next word; a short word is
  the easiest thing in the world to lose. This requires no motive at all.
- **אוֹר was added** by scribes filling a perceived gap, and the Masoretic preserves
  the shorter original.

The first is simpler and explains the grammatical gap. The second cannot be
excluded. **The parchment records neither intent nor direction** — and a
one-word omission is exactly the kind of change that needs no explanation beyond
tiredness.

## The separate variant

**1QIsaa alone reads עַבְדּוֹ, "his servant", against עַבְדִּי, "my servant", in
every other witness including the other three scrolls.** Recorded because it is
there. One witness against four is weak, and this is most likely that scribe's
slip — but it is the manuscript's actual reading and belongs in the record.
