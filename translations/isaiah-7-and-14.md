# Isaiah 7:14 · Isaiah 14:12–15

Translated from the Hebrew. No English translation was consulted or adapted.
Method and restrictions: `translations/OUR-TRANSLATION.md`.

**A note on the witnesses.** Isaiah is the best-attested book in this corpus.
**1QIsaa** — the Great Isaiah Scroll — is a *complete* Isaiah from before the
common era, 24,078 words, roughly a thousand years older than the Leningrad
Codex. A systematic scan (`./tools/diverge.py Isa`) found heavy minor variation
across the book and **no large-scale rewriting**: not one of Isaiah's unique
readings is a missing sentence.

⚠️ `allwitness.py` will not return the scrolls for Isaiah — the scrolls corpus
abbreviates it **`Is`** where the Masoretic corpus uses **`Isa`**. The readings
below were pulled by querying the node tables directly. This is the same
abbreviation mismatch that once made a divergence scan silently skip the largest
biblical scroll in existence.

# Isaiah 7:14

## Word by word

**לָכֵן** · `ל/כן` · `HR/D` — preposition לְ + adverb כֵּן
> כֵּן: *thus · so · rightly*. With לְ: **"therefore"**.

**יִתֵּן** · `יתן` · `HVqi3ms` — verb, qal, imperfect, 3rd masc sing
> Root **נתן**: *to give · to put · to set · to permit · to appoint*. Very wide.
> Imperfect here is future-oriented: **"will give"**.

**אֲדֹנָי** · `אדנ/י` · `HNcmpc/Sp1cs` — noun, masc plural construct + 1cs suffix
> **אָדוֹן** is *lord, master, owner*. The plural-with-suffix form אֲדֹנָי functions
> as a divine title. Note the form is literally "my lords" — a plural of majesty,
> not a numerical plural.
> **"the Lord"** — but see the variant below; **1QIsaa does not read this word.**

**הוּא** · `הוא` · `HPp3ms` — independent pronoun, 3rd masc sing
> Emphatic, resuming the subject: **"he himself"**.

**לָכֶם** · `ל/כם` · `HR/Sp2mp` — preposition + 2nd masc **plural** suffix
> **"to you"** — plural. The sign is given to a group, not to the king alone.

**אוֹת** · `אות` · `HNcbsa` — noun, common, singular absolute
> Root uncertain. Range: *sign · token · mark · standard · omen · portent*.
> A thing that signifies something beyond itself. **"a sign"**.

**הִנֵּה** · `הנה` — presentative particle
> Directs attention to what is present or imminent: **"look" / "here is"**.
> Not itself a time-marker.

**הָעַלְמָה** · `ה/עלמה` — definite article + noun, feminine singular
> **עַלְמָה**. This is the contested word, so state it precisely.
>
> It denotes **a young woman of marriageable age**. What it specifies is *stage of
> life*; what it does **not** specify is sexual status. The Hebrew word that does
> carry that meaning is **בְּתוּלָה**, and it is not the word used here.
>
> Its other occurrences: Gen 24:43 (Rebekah at the well), Exod 2:8 (Moses' sister),
> Ps 68:26, Prov 30:19, Song 1:3, 6:8. Across these it consistently marks youth and
> unmarried status, and in no case does the text turn on virginity as such.
>
> Range: *young woman · girl of marriageable age · maiden*.
>
> **The definite article matters.** הָעַלְמָה is "**the** young woman" — a specific,
> identifiable person, presumably known to speaker and hearer. Not "a virgin".
> **"the young woman"**

**הָרָה** · `הרה` — feminine singular, form ambiguous between adjective and perfect
> Root **הרה**: to conceive, be pregnant.
> **The parse is genuinely open.** As an *adjective* ("pregnant") it describes a
> present state — she is already with child. As a *perfect verb* ("she has
> conceived") it reports a completed act. Both are morphologically available and
> the pointing does not settle it.
> Rendered here as the state: **"is pregnant"** — with the alternative noted.

**וְיֹלֶדֶת** · `ו/ילדת` · `HC/Vqrfsa` — conjunction + qal **participle**, fem sing
> Root **ילד**: *to bear · to bring forth · to beget*. A participle, so the action
> is ongoing or imminent rather than located in past or future.
> **"and is bearing" / "and about to bear"**

**בֵּן** · `בן` · `HNcmsa` — noun, masc sing absolute
> **בֵּן**: *son · child · descendant · member of a class*. **"a son"**

**וְקָרָאת** · `ו/קראת` · `HC/Vqq3fs` — conjunction + qal, **3rd feminine singular**
> Root **קרא**: *to call · to name · to summon · to proclaim · to read aloud*.
> Third feminine singular: **she** will call. **"and she shall call"**
> ⚠️ **1QIsaa reads וקרא — 3rd masculine singular.** See below.

**שְׁמוֹ** · `שמ/ו` · `HNcmsc/Sp3ms` — noun construct + 3ms suffix. **"his name"**

**עִמָּנוּ אֵל** · `עמנו אל` · `HNp` — parsed as a proper noun, but transparent
> **עִמָּנוּ** = "with us" (preposition עִם + 1cp suffix). **אֵל** = "God".
> The name is a sentence: **"With-us-God"**. Left as **"Immanuel"** with the sense
> given, since the Hebrew hears it as both name and statement.

## The verse — Masoretic text (Leningrad Codex, 1008 CE)

> **Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign: look — the young woman is
> pregnant and bearing a son, and she shall call his name Immanuel.**

*Alternative parse of הָרָה:* "…the young woman has conceived and is bearing a son…"

## The witnesses

**4Q65** matches the Masoretic text at every word of this verse.

**1QIsaa** (the complete pre-Christian scroll) differs in **two** places:

| | Masoretic (1008 CE) | 1QIsaa (pre-CE) |
|---|---|---|
| the giver | **אדני** — "the Lord" | **יהוה** — the divine name |
| who names | **וקראת** — *she* shall call | **וקרא** — *he* shall call (3ms) |

> **1QIsaa: Therefore YHWH himself will give you a sign: look — the young woman is
> pregnant and bearing a son, and he shall call his name Immanuel.**

Both are real variants. Neither touches עַלְמָה: **the scrolls and the Masoretic
text agree exactly on the contested word.** That is a null result and it is worth
as much as a difference — whatever happened in the history of this verse's
interpretation, it did not happen by changing this Hebrew word.

## The Septuagint

The Greek renders עַלְמָה as **παρθένος**. Two things need saying plainly, and
separately:

1. This is a **translator's choice**, made **c. 250–100 BCE** — before any Christian
   reading existed. Whatever drove it, it was not that.
2. παρθένος in Greek of this period is not a rigid technical term either; it too
   can denote a young unmarried woman. It leans further toward virginity than
   עַלְמָה does, but it is a shift of emphasis, not the importing of a word the
   Hebrew lacks a counterpart for — Hebrew *has* בְּתוּלָה and this verse does not
   use it.

What the evidence supports: the Hebrew says *young woman*; a pre-Christian Greek
translator chose a word that leans further. What it does not support: any claim
about intent on either side.

# Isaiah 14:12–15

## Who is being addressed

Established by the passage's own frame, not by inference: **v.4** directs this
taunt-song `עַל־מֶלֶךְ בָּבֶל` — **against the king of Babylon**. The Hebrew names
its addressee.

## Word by word — verse 12

**אֵיךְ** · `איך` — interrogative adverb, used in laments. **"How—!"**

**נָפַלְתָּ** · `נפלת` — qal perfect, **2nd masc sing**
> Root **נפל**: *to fall · to fall down · to be cast down · to fail*.
> Second person: the figure is addressed directly. **"you have fallen"**

**מִשָּׁמַיִם** · `מ/שמים` — preposition מִן + שָׁמַיִם (dual/plural in form)
> **"from the heavens"** — also simply "from the sky"; Hebrew does not distinguish.

**הֵילֵל** · `הילל` — the contested word
> **The root is disputed and the honest answer is that it is not settled.**
> - From **הלל** — *to shine*; the same root also yields *to praise* and, in another
>   stem, *to boast*. This gives **"Shining One" / "Bright One"**.
> - From **ילל** — *to howl, wail*. This gives **"Howler"**, which suits a taunt-song.
>
> The consonants support both. Most read it as the shining one, on the strength of
> the parallel with "son of dawn" — a reasonable argument from context, not proof
> from morphology.
>
> **"Lucifer" is not in this text.** That is the Latin Vulgate's rendering —
> *lucifer*, "light-bringer", the ordinary Latin word for the morning star — which
> passed into English as though it were a proper name. The Hebrew has no proper
> name here.

**בֶּן־שָׁחַר** · `בן שחר` — construct chain
> **שַׁחַר**: *dawn · daybreak · the morning*. **"son of dawn"** — i.e. the morning
> star, the last light before sunrise. In Ugaritic literature Shachar is a named
> deity; noted as cultural background (`docs/RESEARCH-ane-parallels.md`), not as a
> claim about this text.

**נִגְדַּעְתָּ** · `נגדעת` — **niphal** perfect 2ms
> Root **גדע**: *to cut down · to hew off · to chop*. Niphal is passive:
> **"you were cut down"** — the imagery is felling a tree.

**לָאָרֶץ** · `ל/ארץ` — **"to the earth" / "to the ground"**

**חוֹלֵשׁ עַל־גּוֹיִם** · `חולש על גוים`
> **חלש**: *to weaken · to prostrate · to lay low*. Participle.
> **"you who laid nations low"**

## The verses

> **12. How you have fallen from the heavens, Shining One, son of dawn!
> You were cut down to the ground, you who laid the nations low.**
>
> **13. And you — you said in your heart: "I will go up to the heavens; above the
> stars of God I will raise my throne; and I will sit on the mount of assembly, in
> the far reaches of Zaphon.**
>
> **14. I will go up above the high places of the cloud; I will make myself like
> the Most High."**
>
> **15. Yet to Sheol you are brought down, to the far reaches of the pit.**

**הַר־מוֹעֵד** (v.13) is the *mount of assembly* — מוֹעֵד is a meeting or an
appointed gathering. **צָפוֹן** (Zaphon) is both the ordinary word for "north" and
the name of the mountain that is Baal's seat in Ugaritic texts. Both senses are
live; the phrase "far reaches of Zaphon" is at home in either.

**עֶלְיוֹן** in v.14 is the same title as Deuteronomy 32:8 — see
`translations/OUR-TRANSLATION.md`.

## The witnesses at 14:12

| | Reading |
|---|---|
| **Masoretic** (1008 CE) | איך · הילל · נגדעת · על גוים |
| **4Q59** | איך · הילל · נגדעת · על **גוים** — matches |
| **1QIsaa** | **היכה** · **היליל** · **נגדעתה** · על **גוי** (singular) |

1QIsaa's differences are **orthographic** — fuller spellings with final ה, and
היליל for הילל — except the last: **גוי singular** against **גוים plural**. That
one changes the sense from "nations" to "a nation".

Nothing in either scroll turns הֵילֵל into a name, and nothing supplies a proper
noun. The Hebrew witnesses, a thousand years apart, agree on that.
