# Psalm 22:16–17

Translated from the Hebrew. No English translation was consulted or adapted.
Method: `translations/OUR-TRANSLATION.md`.

**Numbering.** Hebrew and English number this psalm one apart, because the Hebrew
counts the superscription as verse 1. **Hebrew 22:17 = English 22:16.** Both are
given below so nothing is lost in the crossing.

This verse carries the sharpest single-word disagreement in the Psalter, and the
witnesses split three ways.

---

## Verse 16 (Heb 17) — the surrounding line, where all witnesses agree

**כִּי** — *for · because · that · when · indeed*. **"for"**

**סְבָבוּנִי** · `סבבו/ני` · `HVqp3cp/Sp1cs` — qal perfect 3rd common plural + 1cs suffix
> Root **סבב**: *to turn about · to surround · to encircle · to go around*.
> **"they have surrounded me"**

**כְּלָבִים** · `כלבים` · `HNcmpa` — noun, masc plural absolute
> **כֶּלֶב**: *dog*. In Biblical usage dogs are scavengers, not companions; the word
> carries contempt. **"dogs"**

**עֲדַת** · `עדת` · `HNcfsc` — noun, fem sing **construct**
> **עֵדָה**: *assembly · congregation · company · swarm*. The same word used of
> Israel's congregation, and — see `translations/psalm-82.md` — of the divine
> assembly at Ps 82:1. **"a company of"**

**מְרֵעִים** · `מרעים` · `HVhrmpa` — hiphil participle, masc plural
> Root **רעע**: *to be evil · to do harm*. Hiphil participle: those who *do* evil.
> **"evildoers"**

**הִקִּיפוּנִי** · `הקיפו/ני` · `HVhp3cp/Sp1cs` — hiphil perfect 3cp + 1cs suffix
> Root **נקף**: *to encircle · to enclose · to go round*. **"have closed in on me"**

## The contested word

Then comes one word, and the witnesses do not agree on what it is.

| Witness | Date | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **4Q88** (4QPsᶠ) | c. 50 BCE | **כרו** — *karu* |
| **5/6HevPs** | c. 50–68 CE | **כארו** — *ka'aru* |
| **Septuagint** | transl. c. 250–100 BCE | **ὤρυξαν** — a finite verb, 3rd plural |
| **Masoretic** (Leningrad) | 1008 CE | **כָּאֲרִי** — *ka'ari* |

### What each reading is, grammatically

**כָּאֲרִי** (Masoretic) — this parses as the preposition **כְּ** ("like") + **אֲרִי**
("lion"): **"like a lion"**.
> This creates a real grammatical problem, and it should be stated rather than
> smoothed over: the clause then has **no verb** governing "my hands and my feet."
> The line reads "…like a lion my hands and my feet," which is not ordinary Hebrew.
> Translations that render it "like a lion, *they are at* my hands and my feet"
> are supplying a verb the Hebrew does not contain.

**כרו** (4Q88) — no aleph. This is a **verb**: qal perfect 3rd common plural of
**כרה**.
> **כרה**: *to dig · to bore · to hollow out · to excavate*. Used of digging a well
> (Gen 26:25), digging a pit (Ps 7:16), hewing out a tomb (Gen 50:5).
> **"they dug" / "they bored"**

**כארו** (5/6HevPs) — with aleph, ending in **waw** rather than the Masoretic **yod**.
> The final letter is the whole dispute. **ו and י are among the most easily
> confused letters in this period's script**, differing chiefly in stroke length —
> so a copyist error in *either* direction is mechanically easy. This form is not a
> straightforward noun; it is most naturally taken as verbal, aligning with 4Q88.

**ὤρυξαν** (Septuagint) — aorist 3rd plural of **ὀρύσσω**.
> **ὀρύσσω**: *to dig · to excavate · to burrow*. The ordinary Greek verb for
> digging earth. **"they dug"**

## The verse — Masoretic text (1008 CE)

> **For dogs have surrounded me; a company of evildoers has closed in on me —
> like a lion, my hands and my feet.**

*(with the grammatical gap left visible, since the Hebrew leaves it)*

## The verse — 4Q88 and the Septuagint tradition

> **For dogs have surrounded me; a company of evildoers has closed in on me;
> they have dug my hands and my feet.**

## What the evidence supports

**A Hebrew scroll from c. 50 BCE and a Greek translation made c. 250–100 BCE both
read a verb, against the noun-phrase of a manuscript from 1008 CE.** Two
independent pre-Christian witnesses agreeing against a much later one — the same
evidential shape as Deuteronomy 32:8, where the scrolls and the Greek agree on
"sons of God" against the Masoretic "sons of Israel."

The verbal reading also removes a grammatical defect: it supplies the predicate the
Masoretic line lacks.

## What the evidence does NOT support

**"Pierced" is not what these words say.**

**כרה** means *to dig, bore, hollow out*. **ὀρύσσω** means *to dig, excavate*.
Neither is the word for piercing or perforating — Hebrew has **דקר** and **נקב**
for that, and Greek has **ἐκκεντέω**, which is exactly the verb used at Zechariah
12:10 and quoted at John 19:37. **None of those words appears here, in any witness.**

So the honest position has two parts, and both are needed:

1. On manuscript grounds the verbal reading — *they dug* — is better supported than
   *like a lion*. Older witnesses, two independent traditions, and it fixes the
   grammar.
2. *They dug* is not *they pierced*. Rendering it "pierced" is an interpretive step
   taken **after** the translation, not a reading of the Hebrew or the Greek. A
   translation that prints "pierced" has made an argument and presented it as a
   text.

**And what nobody can establish from the manuscripts:** intent. Whether the
Masoretic *ka'ari* is a copying slip of one letter, or a deliberate change, or the
older reading with the scrolls being the innovation — the parchment does not say.
The waw/yod confusion runs both directions with equal ease. Anyone who tells you
which way it went with certainty is going beyond the evidence.

## The rest of the line

**יָדַי וְרַגְלָי** · `ידי ו רגלי` — **"my hands and my feet"**
> ⚠️ 5/6HevPs reads **ידיה** here — a *he* ending rather than the expected *yod*.
> Recorded because it is what the scroll has; it is most likely a scribal slip, and
> nothing in the argument above rests on it.
