# Psalm 82

## Why this psalm, and what kind of ambiguity it has

`translations/OUR-TRANSLATION.md` (Deut 32:8) found a **textual** variant: the
Qumran Hebrew and the Septuagint read בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים / ἀγγέλων θεοῦ where the
Masoretic text reads בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל. Three witnesses disagreeing is a
manuscript-level fact.

Psalm 82 is different. `./tools/allwitness.py Ps 82 <verse>` was run for all
eight verses. The Qumran Hebrew (Dead Sea Scrolls) agrees with the Leningrad
Codex **word for word, every verse, no variant** — including at the two most
theologically loaded points, v.6 (אלהים אתם ובני עליון) and v.8 (תנחל בכל
הגוים). `./tools/allwitness.py` has no Septuagint entry wired up for Psalms, so
no LXX comparison is offered here as manuscript evidence (see the note at v.6
about the Greek quotation in John 10:34, which is a different kind of datum —
a 1st-century *citation*, not a variant reading).

So the divine-council-vs-human-judges question in this psalm is **not**
settled or unsettled by a manuscript disagreement the way Deut 32:8 was. It
turns entirely on how the words of the one Hebrew text function grammatically
— construct-chain sense, verb agreement, and lexical range. That is a
different, harder kind of ambiguity, and it is treated word by word below.
Where a single Hebrew phrase genuinely supports more than one sense, the verse
translation gives the most literal rendering with the live alternative
bracketed at that word; the full case for each side is laid out in the closing
section.

## Word by word

### Verse 1

**מִזְמוֹר** `מזמור` `HNcmsa` — noun, common, masc singular absolute. Root
**זמר**, to make music. **"A psalm"**

**לְאָסָף** `ל/אסף` `HR/Np` — preposition לְ ("of, belonging to") + the proper
name **אָסָף**, Asaph (a levitical singer). **"of Asaph"**

**אֱלֹהִים** (1st) `אלהים` `HNcmpa` — noun, common, masc **plural** absolute.
> Root **אלה/אלהים**. As established for this word at Deut 32:8: plural in
> form; takes **singular** agreement when the referent is the God of Israel,
> **plural** agreement when the referent is gods/divine beings generally.
> Range: *God · gods · divine beings · god-like ones.*
> This occurrence is the **subject of נִצָּב**, a *singular* participle (below)
> — the agreement is singular. **"God"**

**נִצָּב** `נצב` `HVNrmsa` — verb, niphal, participle, masc singular absolute.
> Root **נצב** — the same consonantal root as Deut 32:8's יַצֵּב (hiphil, "he
> fixed/set in place"), here in the niphal stative/reflexive: to stand, be
> stationed, take one's stand. Singular participle, agreeing with the singular
> reading of אֱלֹהִים just above.
> Range: *to stand · to be stationed · to take one's stand.* **"stands"**

**בַּעֲדַת** `ב/עדת` `HR/Ncfsc` — preposition בְּ + noun, fem singular
**construct**.
> Root **עדה** (Strong's 5712): an appointed, gathered company — the word used
> for Israel's own congregation (עֲדַת יִשְׂרָאֵל) but also, elsewhere, for a
> company of evildoers (עֲדַת מְרֵעִים, Ps 22:17) or of the wicked. It is a
> general word for a convened body, not restricted to a cultic or Israelite
> sense; construct state, so it must bind to what follows.
> Range: *assembly · congregation · company · gathered body.*
> **"in the assembly/congregation of"**

**אֵל** `אל` `HNcmsa` — noun, common, masc singular absolute.
> Root **אל** (Strong's 410). This word does three distinct things in Biblical
> Hebrew and nothing in the form here picks between them: (1) it is the
> ordinary common noun *"god, deity"*; (2) it is the **proper name** of the
> Canaanite/Ugaritic high god, head of the divine assembly at Ugarit (KTU 1.4,
> where his sons number seventy — see `docs/RESEARCH-ane-parallels.md`); (3) in
> construct after another noun it can function as a **superlative/intensifier**
> — "mountains of El" (הַרְרֵי־אֵל, Ps 36:7) and "cedars of El" (אַרְזֵי־אֵל,
> Ps 80:11) mean "mighty mountains," "mighty cedars." So עֲדַת־אֵל can
> grammatically be: *the assembly of El* (the deity, proper name) · *the
> assembly of God* (generic) · or *the mighty assembly* (superlative). Nothing
> in the construct chain itself selects one. **"El" / "God" / "the mighty" — held open**

**בְּקֶרֶב** `ב/קרב` `HR/Ncmsc` — preposition בְּ + noun, masc singular
construct.
> Root **קרב** (Strong's 7130): the inward part, the midst. Range: *midst ·
> among · inward part.* **"in the midst of"**

**אֱלֹהִים** (2nd) `אלהים` `HNcmpa` — noun, common, masc **plural** absolute,
identical form to the first occurrence.
> No verb agrees directly with this one — it sits inside the prepositional
> phrase בְּקֶרֶב, "in the midst of." That matters: **Hebrew marks a direct
> object with אֶת or leaves it unmarked-accusative; a noun governed by בְּ is
> not grammatically the object of the following verb.** So the text does not
> say, in a straightforward syntactic sense, "he judges the gods" — it says
> "amid gods, he judges," with the *object* of judging left unstated by the
> grammar. Whether "the gods" here means the same referent as the first
> אֱלֹהִים (i.e. this is emphatic repetition, "God stands... in the midst of
> [that same] God/council he judges") or a distinct plural group (other gods,
> among whom the singular God stands) is not settled by the syntax; both are
> live. **"gods" / "God" — referent held open**

**יִשְׁפֹּט** `ישפט` `HVqi3ms` — verb, qal, imperfect, 3ms.
> Root **שפט** (Strong's 8199). Range across its Biblical Hebrew occurrences is
> wider than courtroom adjudication: *to judge (render a legal verdict) · to
> govern, rule, exercise authority (the "judges" of the book of Judges are
> tribal leaders/rulers, not solely magistrates) · to vindicate, execute
> justice for someone.* No object is marked. **"he judges" / "he governs" /
> "he executes judgment"** — subject most naturally the singular God of the
> first אֱלֹהִים, carried through from נִצָּב.

> **God stands in the assembly of El [the mighty assembly / the assembly of
> God]; in the midst of the gods he judges.**

### Verse 2

as* + *when.* **"How long"**

**תִּשְׁפְּטוּ** `תשפטו` `HVqi2mp` — qal imperfect 2mp, root שפט (as above).
**"will you judge"**

**עָוֶל** `עול` `HNcbsa` — noun, common, either-gender singular absolute. Root

**וּפְנֵי** `ו/פני` `HC/Ncbpc` — conjunction + noun, construct plural. **"and
the faces of"**

**רְשָׁעִים** `רשעים` `HAampa` — adjective, masc plural absolute, substantized.

**תִּשְׂאוּ** `תשאו` `HVqi2mp` — qal imperfect 2mp.
> Root **נשא** (Strong's 5375): to lift, carry, bear, take up. The collocation
> "lift the face of (someone)" is an established Hebrew idiom for showing

**סֶלָה** `סלה` `HTj` — a liturgical/musical marker of uncertain etymology and

> **How long will you judge unjustly and show partiality to the wicked?
> Selah.**

### Verse 3

**שִׁפְטוּ** `שפטו` `HVqv2mp` — qal imperative 2mp, root שפט. **"Judge"**

**דַל** `דל` `HAamsa` — adjective. Root **דלל**, to be low/thin. Range: *poor ·
low · weak.* **"the poor"**

**וְיָתוֹם** `ו/יתום` `HC/Ncmsa` — conjunction + noun. **"and the orphan"**

**עָנִי** `עני` `HAamsa` — adjective. Range: *poor · afflicted · humble ·
lowly.* **"the afflicted"**

**וָרָשׁ** `ו/רש` `HC/Vqrmsa` — conjunction + qal participle, root **רוש/ריש**,

**הַצְדִּיקוּ** `הצדיקו` `HVhv2mp` — hiphil imperative 2mp.
> Root **צדק**, to be right/righteous. Hiphil here is declarative/causative:
> to cause to be in the right, vindicate, do justice for. **"vindicate"**

> **Judge the poor and the orphan; vindicate the afflicted and the
> destitute.**

### Verse 4

**פַּלְּטוּ** `פלטו` `HVpv2mp` — piel imperative 2mp, root **פלט**, to escape
→ (piel) to bring to safety, rescue. **"Rescue"**

**דַל וְאֶבְיוֹן** `דל/ו/אביון` — "the poor and needy" (אֶבְיוֹן, Strong's
34, adjective: *needy, poor, destitute*).

**מִיַּד** `מ/יד` `HR/Ncbsc` — preposition + construct noun **יָד**, "hand."
**"from the hand of"**

**רְשָׁעִים** — "the wicked," as v.2.

**הַצִּילוּ** `הצילו` `HVhv2mp` — hiphil imperative 2mp, root **נצל**, to
snatch away → (hiphil) deliver, rescue. **"deliver"**

> **Rescue the poor and needy; deliver them from the hand of the wicked.**

### Verse 5

**לֹא יָדְעוּ** `לא/ידעו` `HTn/HVqp3cp` — negative particle + qal perfect
3cp, root ידע, "to know." **"They do not know"**

**וְלֹא יָבִינוּ** — conjunction + negative + qal imperfect 3mp, root **בין**,
"to discern, understand." **"and do not understand"**

**בַּחֲשֵׁכָה** `ב/חשכה` `HR/Ncfsa` — preposition + noun, "darkness" (Strong's
2825). **"in darkness"**

**יִתְהַלָּכוּ** `יתהלכו` `HVti3mp` — hithpael imperfect 3mp, root **הלך**,
"to walk"; hithpael is the iterative/reflexive stem — "walk about, go back and
forth." **"they walk about"**

**יִמּוֹטוּ** `ימוטו` `HVNi3mp` — niphal imperfect 3mp, root **מוט** (Strong's
4131), "to totter, slip, be shaken." **"totter"**

**כָּל־מוֹסְדֵי** `כל/מוסדי` `HNcmsc/HNcbpc` — "all the foundations of," from
**יסד**, "to found, establish."

**אָרֶץ** `ארץ` `HNcbsa` — "earth, land." **"the earth"**

> **They do not know, and they do not understand; they walk about in
> darkness. All the foundations of the earth totter.**

*Who is "they"?* The verbs are bare 3mp — Hebrew supplies no noun here, only
verb morphology. Grammatically the antecedent could be **הָרְשָׁעִים**, "the
wicked" of vv.2–4 (matching gender and number), or it could look **forward**
to the אֱלֹהִים addressed in v.6 — i.e., the speech that begins "I said, you
are gods" in v.6 is the direct-address continuation of an indictment about
these same figures' ignorance in v.5. Both are grammatically live; the verse
does not name its subject.

### Verse 6

**אֲנִי־אָמַרְתִּי** `אני/אמרתי` — pronoun 1cs + qal perfect 1cs, root **אמר**.
**"I said"**

**אֱלֹהִים** `אלהים` `HNcmpa` — noun, masc plural absolute, predicate (no
copula verb in Hebrew — supplied by context: "[you are] gods").
> Same word, same form as both occurrences in v.1. Here the addressee is
> explicitly **2mp** (אַתֶּם, next word) — a genuine plural subject taking a
> genuine plural predicate, unlike v.1's first occurrence (singular referent
> via נִצָּב's agreement). **"gods"**

**אַתֶּם** `אתם` `HPp2mp` — pronoun, 2mp. **"you (plural) are"**

**וּבְנֵי** `ו/בני` `HC/Ncmpc` — conjunction + construct plural, root **בן**,
"son." Construct: *sons of.*

**עֶלְיוֹן** `עליון` `HAamsa` — adjective, masc singular absolute, functioning
as a title.
> As established at Deut 32:8: from **עלה**, "to go up." Range: *upper ·
> uppermost · Most High.* Here a divine title in the construct chain. This is
> the same **בְּנֵי + עֶלְיוֹן** collocation-type as Deut 32:8's disputed
> בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים/אלוה — "sons of the Most High," the divine-sonship idiom
> this project already documented there. **"sons of the Most High" / "sons of Elyon"**

**כֻּלְּכֶם** `כל/כם` `HNcmsc/Sp2mp` — construct noun **כֹּל** ("all, the
whole of") + suffix 2mp. **"all of you"**

> **I said, "You are gods, and sons of the Most High are all of you."**

*The Qumran Hebrew (`./tools/allwitness.py Ps 82 6`) reads אלהים אתם ובני
עליון כל׳כם — identical to the Leningrad Codex.* No textual variant here.

**A datum, not a translation basis:** this verse is quoted in Greek at John
10:34 — `Ἐγὼ εἶπα· Θεοί ἐστε;` ("I said, You are gods?") — and again referenced
in 10:35, `εἰ ἐκείνους εἶπεν θεοὺς πρὸς οὓς ὁ λόγος τοῦ θεοῦ ἐγένετο` ("if he
called them gods, to whom the word of God came") (Greek text: `greek-nt/SBLGNT/data/sblgnt/text/John.txt`).
This is a 1st-century-CE reading of the verse, not a manuscript variant of the
psalm — it is offered here only as evidence of ancient reception: someone in
the 1st century read אֱלֹהִים in this verse as θεοί, plural "gods," not as a
word for human "judges" or "rulers" (κριταί/ἄρχοντες), and used that reading in
a legal argument about the term's applicability. It is data about *how the
phrase was once read*, not an argument for what it means; the Hebrew is
translated above on its own grammar, independent of it.

### Verse 7

**אָכֵן** `אכן` `HD` — adverb. Range: *surely · indeed · nevertheless.*
**"Surely"**

**כְּאָדָם** `כ/אדם` `HR/Ncmsa` — preposition כְּ ("like, as") + noun, masc
singular absolute, **no article**.
> Root **אדם** (Strong's 120). As established at Deut 32:8: *humankind
> collectively, a human being; also the name Adam.* The absence of the article
> does not decide this — proper names in Hebrew regularly appear without the
> article anyway, so its absence neither confirms nor rules out the
> proper-name reading. Two live readings: **"like a man / like a mortal /
> like humankind"** (generic, stressing ordinary human mortality) or **"like
> Adam"** (the first man, under the specific death sentence of Gen 2:17/3:19,
> מוֹת תָּמוּת — "you shall surely die," the same root מות used in the very
> next word here). The literary echo with Genesis's death sentence is real
> either way the proper noun is read grammatically or not.

**תְּמוּתוּן** `תמותו/ן` `HVqi2mp/Sn` — qal imperfect 2mp + paragogic נ.
Root **מות**, "to die." **"you will die"**

**וּכְאַחַד** `ו/כ/אחד` `HC/R/Acmsc` — conjunction + preposition + numeral,
construct. **"and like one of"**

**הַשָּׂרִים** `ה/שרים` `HTd/Ncmpa` — article + noun, masc plural.
> Root **שר** (Strong's 8269). Its range across Biblical Hebrew occurrences
> covers both human officials (*chief, official, captain, prince*) **and**
> non-human celestial figures — Daniel uses שַׂר for patron/guardian
> beings attached to nations (שַׂר מַלְכוּת פָּרַס, "the prince of the
> kingdom of Persia," Dan 10:13; מִיכָאֵל שַׂרְכֶם, "Michael your prince,"
> Dan 10:21), and Joshua 5:14 has שַׂר־צְבָא־יְהוָה, "commander of YHWH's
> army," for a supernatural figure. So "like one of the שָׂרִים" can be read
> as "like one of the [earthly] princes/officials" or "like one of the
> [heavenly] princes" — the word does not specify which on its own.
> **"the princes/officials"**

**תִּפֹּלוּ** `תפלו` `HVqi2mp` — qal imperfect 2mp, root **נפל**, "to fall."
**"you will fall"**

> **Surely you will die like a man [like Adam], and fall like one of the
> princes.**

### Verse 8

**קוּמָה** `קומ/ה` `HVqv2ms/Sh` — qal imperative 2ms + emphatic ה. Root
**קום**, "to rise, stand up." **"Arise"**

**אֱלֹהִים** `אלהים` `HNcmpa` — vocative, direct address: **"O God"** — the
same singular-referent use as v.1's first occurrence and v.8's addressee is
the one being *called on* to act, not a plural group.

**שָׁפְטָה** `שפט/ה` `HVqv2ms/Sh` — qal imperative 2ms + emphatic ה, root שפט
(as above). **"judge"**

**הָאָרֶץ** `ה/ארץ` `HTd/Ncbsa` — article + noun. **"the earth"**

**כִּי** `כי` `HC` — conjunction. *for · because · that · indeed.* **"for"**

**אַתָּה** `אתה` `HPp2ms` — pronoun 2ms. **"you"**

**תִנְחַל** `תנחל` `HVqi2ms` — qal imperfect 2ms.
> Root **נחל** (Strong's 5157) — **the same root that opens the Deut 32:8
> entry above** (בְּהַנְחֵל, hiphil, "when he apportioned") and closes 32:9
> (נַחֲלָתוֹ, "his inheritance"). There the base sense established was: *to
> take or hold as an allotted portion — property that comes by right and
> division rather than purchase.* Here it is **qal**, not hiphil — not
> causing another to hold a portion, but taking/holding a portion oneself.
> Range, consistent with the earlier entry: *to inherit · to take possession
> of · to hold as one's allotted portion.* **"you shall take as your own
> portion" / "you shall inherit"**

**בְּכָל־הַגּוֹיִם** `ב/כל/ה/גוים` — preposition + "all" + article + **גּוֹיִם**
(same word and range as Deut 32:8: *nation · people as a body · gentiles*).
**"all the nations"**

> **Arise, O God, judge the earth, for you shall take as your own portion
> all the nations.**

*The Qumran Hebrew (`./tools/allwitness.py Ps 82 8`) reads קומ׳ה אלהים שפט׳ה
ה ארץ כי אתה תנחל ב כל ה גוים — identical to the Leningrad Codex.* No variant.

**The נחל link to Deut 32:8–9, stated plainly:** in Deut 32:8–9 (on the older,
non-Masoretic reading already documented in this corpus), Elyon apportions
(נחל, hiphil) the nations among divine sons, and YHWH's own allotted portion
(נַחֲלָה) turns out to be Jacob alone — one nation among the rest. Psalm 82:8
closes with the *same root*, qal: the psalmist calls on God to "take as
portion" (תִנְחַל) **all** the nations, not Jacob's share only. If the two
texts are read together, Ps 82:8 reads as a plea to reverse or supersede the
original apportionment of 32:8 — the God who received one nation as his נַחֲלָה
is asked to take all of them. That is a literary/thematic connection through a
shared root, not a manuscript-level claim; nothing here says the two poems
were composed together or by the same hand.

## Two readings, neither declared a winner

The psalm is read two ways in the scholarship, both with real defenders, and
the Hebrew grammar by itself does not close the question. What follows is the
evidence for each, stated as evidence, not as a verdict.

### The case for a divine council

- **v.1's grammar tracks two distinct referents of אֱלֹהִים in one verse**: the
  first occurrence takes singular verbal agreement (נִצָּב is a singular
  participle) — one figure, "God," standing. The second occurrence sits inside
  "in the midst of" — a group, plural, that this singular God stands among.
  Same word, same form, different number of referents in the space of one
  verse — exactly the kind of double-reference the KJV-style "God" reading
  flattens.
- **עֲדַת־אֵל can name El's own assembly** — the Ugaritic high god whose
  council of (seventy) sons is attested at Ugarit (KTU 1.4; see
  `docs/RESEARCH-ane-parallels.md`), the same background this project already
  invoked for Deut 32:8's בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים.
- **v.6's אֱלֹהִים...וּבְנֵי עֶלְיוֹן is the same "sons of the Most High" idiom
  as Deut 32:8** — plural, second person, addressed directly as a group,
  called both "gods" and "sons of Elyon" in the same breath. If בְּנֵי עֶלְיוֹן
  is divine sonship language in Deut 32:8 (which this corpus's own DSS/LXX
  evidence supports), the identical phrase here carries the same weight.
- **v.7's "you will die like a man"** only has force as a *reversal* if the
  addressees were not mortal by default — condemning a mortal judge to
  ordinary human death is not much of a sentence; condemning a being who
  seemed to be more than that, to die "like a man," is a demotion. שָׂרִים in
  the same verse has an attested celestial-being sense elsewhere (Dan 10).
- **v.8's closing appeal, "arise... judge... take all the nations as your
  portion,"** matches the shape of Deut 32:8–9 exactly if that passage is read
  on its older attested text: multiple divine sons each held a נַחֲלָה of
  nations; here the psalmist asks the one true God to take them *all*.

### The case for human judges

- **שפט's own range includes "govern, rule"**, not only supernatural
  adjudication — the term "judges" throughout the Hebrew Bible (Deborah,
  Gideon, Samson) means human tribal leaders/rulers. A council of unjust human
  magistrates ("how long will you judge unjustly") fits the vocabulary just as
  well.
- **אֵל in construct after a noun is independently attested as a superlative
  intensifier** (הַרְרֵי־אֵל, אַרְזֵי־אֵל) — עֲדַת־אֵל could mean simply "the
  mighty/solemn assembly," describing a formal human court with no deity
  named in it at all.
- **אֱלֹהִים is used of human authorities elsewhere in the Hebrew Bible**
  (compare its use for those who exercise God-derived judicial authority in
  passages like Exodus's case laws) — an attested, if contested, semantic
  range, not an invention.
- **v.2–4's content is entirely about social justice among human litigants** —
  the poor, the orphan, the afflicted, the destitute, rescue "from the hand of
  the wicked." Every concrete grievance named is the kind of thing a human
  court, not a cosmic council, adjudicates.
- **v.7's "like one of the שָׂרִים"** — the ordinary, far more frequent sense of
  שַׂר in the Hebrew Bible is a human official/prince, not a celestial being;
  reading it as "you'll die like any other official" (i.e. despite their
  claim to special, God-derived authority, they are as mortal as anyone) is
  the more economical reading of the word's normal usage.

### Where the Hebrew genuinely underdetermines it

- **בְּקֶרֶב אֱלֹהִים יִשְׁפֹּט (v.1) never grammatically marks "gods" as the
  object of judgment.** The verse can mean "God judges, positioned among
  [other] gods" or "God judges, positioned among [a council called] gods,"
  without specifying that the gods are what's being judged — that has to be
  inferred from v.6–7's later direct address, not read directly off v.1's
  syntax.
- **עֲדַת־אֵל (v.1) is compatible with three distinct senses** on construct-
  chain grammar alone (proper name, common noun, superlative) and nothing in
  the four-word phrase itself selects one.
- **אֱלֹהִים as a word never distinguishes "a deity" from "one who exercises
  divinely-derived authority"** by its own morphology; that distinction is
  read into the word from context and theology, not out of the plural noun
  form itself, which is identical either way.
- **כְּאָדָם (v.7) is genuinely ambiguous between "like a human" and "like
  Adam"** on form alone — no article either way is diagnostic.
- **The psalm gives no third-person narrative frame** that identifies the
  addressees of v.6 by name, species, or office — they are simply "you," a
  2mp pronoun, and the reader supplies who that is from the rest of the poem
  and from prior conviction about what such language can mean.

No manuscript variant breaks the tie here the way 4Q37 broke it for Deut 32:8.
This is a case where two coherent, internally consistent readings sit on
exactly the same consonants, and the text does not adjudicate between them.
