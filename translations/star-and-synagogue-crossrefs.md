# Companion to star-and-synagogue-study.md — everywhere else the Bible calls this out

**REPORT / OURS.** Every verse below was regenerated from the texts on disk in
`~/projects/felz-work`:
- Hebrew: `hebrew-ot/morphhb/wlc` (WLC + OSHB morphology), all 39 books, 23,213 verses
- Greek: `greek-nt/morphgnt` (SBLGNT text + morphology)

Method: lemma co-occurrence scan — a celestial lemma (3556 star, 8121 sun, 3394 moon,
4208 constellations, 4216 Mazzaroth, 4446 queen-of-heaven, 5522 Sakkuth, 3594 Kaiwan, or
6635+8064 "host of heaven" in one verse) together with a cult lemma (7812 bow/worship,
5647 serve, 6999 burn incense, 5258 libation, 2076 sacrifice), then a second targeted pass.
Control verse Deut 4:19 confirmed present before trusting the output.

No English translation was consulted. Where this file gives an English sense it is marked as
a gloss of the Hebrew/Greek word, not a quotation of any version.

## 2. The deliberate contrasts — same words, opposite direction

1. **CONFIRMED — Sakkuth and Kaiwan are proper nouns.** The study inferred this from
   commentary; the morphology dataset tags both **HNp**, proper noun, independently. The
   claim now rests on the dataset, not on a secondary source.

2. **CORRECTION — "the star of your god" is plural.** אֱלֹהֵיכֶם is **HNcmpc**, masculine
   **plural** construct: "the star of your **gods**." The study inherited KJV's singular.
   Small in English, but it changes the verse from one deity to a set.

3. **FLAGGED — "your Moloch" is not settled in the data itself.** מַלְכְּכֶם carries lemma
   **4432 (Molech)** but morph **HNcmsc/Sp2mp** — a *common* noun in construct with a 2mp
   suffix, i.e. "your **king**." The lexicon and the morphology of the same dataset disagree
   on this word. The study presents Moloch as given; it is a reading, and the alternative
   ("your king") is what the grammar tags actually encode.

4. **OPEN — the tense is genuinely ambiguous and the study doesn't mention it.**
   וּנְשָׂאתֶם is **HC/Vqq2mp**, waw + qal *sequential* perfect. That form normally carries
   forward as a consequential/future ("and you shall carry…"), which would attach v.26 to the
   sentence of exile in v.27 rather than making it a charge about the wilderness generation.
   KJV ("ye have borne") and Acts 7:43 (ἀνελάβετε, aorist) both take the past reading. Both
   readings are grammatically available; neither should be declared the winner.

5. **CONFIRMED — no shape word exists in the verse.** The only descriptor is כּוֹכַב, "star."
   There is no term for shape, point-count, or geometry anywhere in Amos 5:26. The study's
   §5 conclusion holds, and now holds on an exhaustive word list rather than an assertion.

6. **RESOLVED — see §5 below.** The Septuagint has since been added to the local checkout
   and the chain verified from both sides.

## 5. The Rephan chain — VERIFIED

`git sparse-checkout add septuagint/LXX-Rahlfs-1935/01_wordlist_unicode` and
`septuagint/LXX-Swete/texts/DONE`. Both sides of the chain are now on disk.

### The Greek of Amos 5:26, two independent editions

**Rahlfs 1935** (word range 491674–491692, verse index from `alignment_with_OSSP/E-verse.csv`):

> καὶ ἀνελάβετε τὴν σκηνὴν τοῦ Μολοχ καὶ τὸ ἄστρον τοῦ θεοῦ ὑμῶν Ραιφαν, τοὺς τύπους αὐτῶν
> οὓς ἐποιήσατε ἑαυτοῖς

**Swete 1887–94** (diplomatic, Vaticanus-based), `37.Amos.txt` line 37.5.26:

> καὶ ἀνελάβετε τὴν σκηνὴν τοῦ Μόλοχ καὶ τὸ ἄστρον τοῦ θεοῦ ὑμῶν Ῥαιφάν, τοὺς τύπους αὐτῶν
> οὓς ἐποιήσατε ἑαυτοῖς·

The two editions agree. And LXX Amos 5:27 reads **ἐπέκεινα Δαμασκοῦ**.

### CONFIRMED 1 — Ραιφαν stands exactly where the Hebrew has כִּיּוּן

Regenerated from both texts on disk, not from commentary. The substitution is real.

### CONFIRMED 2 — Acts 7:43 is quoting the Greek, not the Hebrew

| | LXX Amos 5:26–27 | Acts 7:43 (SBLGNT) |
|---|---|---|
| verb | καὶ ἀνελάβετε | καὶ ἀνελάβετε |
| | τὴν σκηνὴν τοῦ Μολοχ | τὴν σκηνὴν τοῦ Μολὸχ |
| | καὶ τὸ ἄστρον τοῦ θεοῦ | καὶ τὸ ἄστρον τοῦ θεοῦ |
| the name | ὑμῶν Ραιφαν | Ῥαιφάν *(ὑμῶν varies — see below)* |
| | τοὺς τύπους **αὐτῶν** | τοὺς τύπους |
| | οὓς ἐποιήσατε | οὓς ἐποιήσατε |
| purpose | **ἑαυτοῖς** "for yourselves" | **προσκυνεῖν αὐτοῖς** "to worship them" |
| sentence | καὶ μετοικιῶ ὑμᾶς ἐπέκεινα | καὶ μετοικιῶ ὑμᾶς ἐπέκεινα |
| destination | **Δαμασκοῦ** | **Βαβυλῶνος** |

The rare form **μετοικιῶ** is shared verbatim. This is quotation of the Greek, full stop.
So the study's §2 claims both hold, and the Damascus→Babylon substitution is now confirmed
against the Greek Vorlage as well as the Hebrew — Stephen is not following a different Hebrew
text, he is changing the Greek he is quoting.

### ⭐ The manuscripts, read directly (CNTR transcriptions, `class 1/`)

| GA | Manuscript | Reads |
|---|---|---|
| **01** | Sinaiticus (c. 350) | του θυ **υμων ρομφαν** |
| **02** | Alexandrinus (5th c.) | του θυ **υμων ραιφαν** |
| **03** | Vaticanus (c. 300–325) | του θυ **ρομφα** *(no υμων)* |
| **04** | Ephraemi (5th c.) | του θυ **υμων ρεφαν** |
| **05** | Bezae (c. 400) | του θυ **ρεμφαμ** *(no υμων)* |

**Five manuscripts, five spellings of the name.** The SBLGNT apparatus records four —
`Ῥαιφάν NA28 ] Ῥομφά WH; Ῥεφάν Treg; Ῥεμφάν RP` — which is four modern editors each
following a different manuscript. **Fourth instance of the pattern already logged in this
repo: an apparatus of editions is not an apparatus of manuscripts.** (Note RP's Ῥεμφάν is not
even exactly Bezae's ρεμφαμ, which ends in mu.)

**On ὑμῶν:** 01, 02 and 04 have it and therefore match the LXX exactly; 03 and 05 lack it.
SBLGNT prints without it because it follows WH/Treg, i.e. Vaticanus and Bezae. This
**corrects an earlier note of mine** that Acts "drops ὑμῶν" — that was one edition's choice
read as the text's.

**⭐ Variant the apparatus does not carry:** Bezae reads **ἐπὶ τὰ μέρη Βαβυλῶνος** — "to the
*regions* of Babylon" — where every other witness has ἐπέκεινα, "beyond." Same shape as the
eight unreported manuscript disagreements found in `translations/revelation-3.md`.

### 🛑 STILL NOT VERIFIED — and the study's mechanism does not reach its own conclusion

The study explains Rephan as a scribal slip: "Hebrew *kaph* (כ) misread as *resh* (ר),
yielding *Rywn* → *Raiphan*."

Lay the consonants out:

```
Hebrew   כיון      =  K - Y - W - N
Greek    Ραιφαν    =  R - AI - PH - A - N
```

כ→ר accounts for the **R**. Nothing in the stated explanation accounts for the **φ**, which
requires a second substitution (ו→פ). The study presents one confusion where the word needs
at least two. That is not a disproof — multi-step corruption in a name is ordinary — but the
explanation as written does not arrive at the word it is explaining, and **no manuscript on
disk shows an intermediate stage.** It is an inference about a lost stage and should be
labelled as one.

Also still open:
- **No LXX apparatus is on disk.** The Rahlfs directory here is a single edition with no
  variant record, and Swete is essentially Vaticanus — so for the Greek side of Amos this is
  closer to one witness than two. Whether Greek Amos itself varied in the name is unchecked.
  The Göttingen Septuagint, which would settle it, is copyrighted.
- **The Coptic/Egyptian Saturn-name alternative** the study mentions as an older suggestion:
  nothing on disk bears on it. Unverified.
