## Why this file exists

The question is whether the astral emblem condemned in Amos 5:26 / Acts 7:43 is the
six-pointed star. The corpus can settle what the *text* says. It cannot settle what
a symbol looked like or when it acquired meaning. That needed outside sources, so
they are quarantined here.

eight-pointed rosette or a radiate mark. The text supplies a category and withholds
the specific.

## 2. Earliest attestations of the hexagram

| Attestation | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Seal of Joshua ben Asayahu, found at Sidon, bearing a hexagram.** Theophoric *-yahu* name — a Yahweh-worshipper's seal. | **7th c. BCE** | Scholem, *Kabbalah*, 362–368 |
| Hexagram in synagogue decoration, incl. the Capernaum stone, **alongside a pentagram and a swastika-like motif** | 3rd–4th c. CE | Scholem 1949; Oegema 1996 |
| Also on ancient churches and mosques, not only synagogues | Antiquity onward | Scholem 1949 |
| Long use in India — Hindu (Lakshmi), Buddhist | Ancient, continuous | Oegema 1996 |

### ⭐ The Sidon seal is the load-bearing item

It places a hexagram in a **Levantine, Yahwistic context roughly a century after
Amos.** Any argument that the hexagram was simply absent from that world is wrong.

**What it does not establish:** that the hexagram was Saturn's emblem, or that it is
what Amos meant. Scholem reads early instances as magical signs or ornament of
unclear meaning, and is explicit that the hexagram was **not a distinctively Jewish
symbol for most of Jewish history.**

---

## 3. The Saturn question — the claim is weaker than usually stated

The popular chain runs: Amos condemns Saturn → the hexagram is Saturn's symbol →
Amos condemned the hexagram. **Join 2 does not hold up in the sources.**

| System | Date | What it assigns to Saturn |
|---|---|---|
| **Agrippa, *De occulta philosophia libri tres*, Bk. II** — foundational text of Western planetary talismanic magic | **1533** | A **3×3 magic square** (numbers 3, 9, 15, 45). Saturn's seal is traced from that square. **Not a hexagram.** |
| **Golden Dawn hexagram rituals** | **1888+** | The hexagram is the **generic figure of the seven classical planets** — six points plus centre. Each planet traced from a different point; Saturn is one of seven. **Not Saturn's own sigil.** |

So in both systems that actually define planetary sigils in the Western tradition,
**Saturn's figure is a square, and the hexagram belongs to the planets
collectively.**

### 🛑 "The hexagram is the talisman of Saturn in Kabbalistic sources"

This circulates widely. In the sources checked it appears on **occult-interest and
religious-polemic websites** — and **not** in Scholem, who studied the symbol for
some fifty years, nor in Oegema's monograph.

That is **not** proof it is absent from primary Kabbalistic material, which was not
searched directly. It does mean the claim is **UNSOURCED** here and should not be
leaned on until someone produces the primary text.

## 4. Later Jewish adoption

| Event | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Known as **"Seal of Solomon"** — decorative and magical sign, Jewish *and* Islamic | Medieval | Scholem 1949; Oegema 1996 |
| Prague Jewish community granted a flag bearing it | **1354** | Scholem 1949 |
| Adopted as a unifying communal emblem — Scholem's argument is that Jews sought a symbol parallel to the cross and the crescent | 18th–19th c. | Scholem 1949 |
| National symbol via the Zionist movement; onto the flag of Israel | 19th–20th c. | Scholem 1949 |

Scholem's overall position: **the hexagram does not originate within Judaism**, and
its career as *the* Jewish symbol is late.

## 5. Chronology

```
c. 760-750 BC   Amos 5:26 — emblem of Saturn-associated deities condemned.
                Called a "star." No point-count given.
    7th c. BC   Seal of Joshua ben Asayahu, Sidon — HEXAGRAM, Yahwistic name.
   c. AD 34     Acts 7:43 — same text quoted in Greek. No point-count.
 3rd-4th c AD   Capernaum stone — hexagram as ornament, beside pentagram
                and a swastika-like motif.
     Medieval   "Seal of Solomon" — Jewish AND Islamic magical/decorative use.
         1354   Prague flag — hexagram as Jewish communal emblem.
         1533   Agrippa: Saturn = 3x3 magic square. NOT a hexagram.
      1888 on   Golden Dawn: hexagram = the seven planets generically.
  19th-20th c   National symbol via Zionism.
       20th c   The Amos-to-hexagram identification appears in print.
```

## 6. Where it lands

Three joins are required:

1. **Amos condemns worship of Saturn-associated deities.** ✅ Established on the
   primary texts.
2. **The hexagram is Saturn's emblem.** ⚠️ Not established. Agrippa gives Saturn a
   square; the Golden Dawn hexagram is generic to all seven planets; no scholarly
   source consulted makes the hexagram Saturn's proper sigil.
3. **Therefore the emblem in Amos is the hexagram.** Requires join 2, and requires
   retrojecting it across roughly 2,300 years.

**Stated neutrally:** Amos names a star and withholds the point-count. A hexagram is
attested in the right region within about a century. Nothing in the primary sources
ties that hexagram to Saturn, and the Saturn–hexagram association that does exist is
documented two millennia later and generically.

Whether that is sufficient is a judgment about method, not about the text.

## 7. What would settle it, and is not held here

- Iconographic evidence placing a **six-pointed** form on Saturn or Kaiwan in the
  8th-century BC Levant. Nothing in this corpus bears on it either way.
- Any **pre-20th-century exegetical tradition** making the Amos→hexagram
  identification. **Not searched. Unverified.**
- A primary source connecting the medieval Saturn–hexagram material *backward* to
  Near Eastern astral cult, rather than arising independently in European magical
  literature.
- **Primary Kabbalistic texts** on hexagram–Saturn. Not searched directly.

## 8. Reception

Revelation 2:9 / 3:9 and the Amos→hexagram identification both circulate in modern
polemical literature, including antisemitic material where the function is to assert
that contemporary Jews worship Saturn. Recorded here as **reception history** — a
fact about how the arguments have been used. **It is not evidence for or against the
claims themselves.**
