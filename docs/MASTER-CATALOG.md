# Master Catalog — 8-agent research pass, 2026-08-08

Consolidates the eight reports in this directory. Every item below was HEAD-verified live on 2026-08-08 by the agent that found it; items that could not be verified are in §5 and are **not** claimed as available.

**Two dates on everything**, because they are different questions:
- **Witness** — when the manuscript the text rests on was physically made.
- **Edition** — when this particular version was published.

---

## §0 — The fact that governs how you read this list

**Erasmus's Greek NT (1516–1535)**, ancestor of the Textus Receptus and the KJV, was set from a handful of **12th–13th century** Basel minuscules. For the last six verses of Revelation, where his one manuscript was damaged, Erasmus **back-translated from the Latin Vulgate into Greek himself**. Those verses stand in no Greek manuscript.

So a 1516 printing can rest on 1200s copies, while a 1935 edition (Rahlfs) reads a 4th-century codex. **Old edition ≠ old witness.** Both columns, always.

Corollary from the gnostic pass: Nag Hammadi was found in **1945**, the Berlin Codex acquired **1896**, Codex Tchacos surfaced in the **1970s**. No pre-1820 edition of any of them can exist — nobody knew they were there. For that material the date rule cannot discriminate at all.

## §5 — Blocked, and why

**Copyright, confirmed:** NA28/UBS5 and ECM (German Bible Society; Acts is the one free exception) · BHS/BHQ apparatus · DJD Dead Sea Scrolls transcriptions · Brill *Coptic Gnostic Library* (complete Nag Hammadi Coptic) · Codex Tchacos / Gospel of Judas (© Maecenas Foundation, pulled from the PDF's own front matter) · Díez Macho's Targum Neofiti · Stuttgart Vulgate · Vetus Latina Database (Brepols) · Leiden Armenian Textbase · Yadin's 1965 Masada Ben Sira (lending only) · Beentjes 1997, Rey & Reymond 2024, Tal's Samaritan Targum.

**Access-gated, not licensed:** naghammadi.org (French translations, reproduction forbidden) · UNESCO Nag Hammadi facsimile (controlled digital lending) · Friedberg Genizah + Cambridge Digital Library (bulk reuse gated) · Trismegistos (paywalled since 2020) · NTVMR (API only, one MS at a time) · **all TITUS Frankfurt texts** (scholarly/attribution terms, *not* an open license — do not assume permission).

**Rejected as laundering, not licensing** — these would have worked; none are recommended:
- A 1972 Berlin Codex edition sitting unrestricted on archive.org, almost certainly still in copyright ("not flagged by IA" is not proof of legality).
- A "Marcion" software bundle claiming the Nag Hammadi Library falls under its app's GPL. The app's license does not cover the texts inside it.
- A third-party mirror of the copyrighted Gospel of Judas transcription.

**Traps caught:**
- **Sefaria "Targum Neofiti" returns HTTP 200 and is 8 verses of Genesis 1.** Found by fetching content, not trusting the status code. Real Neofiti is blocked — Vatican Codex Neofiti 1 forbids reproduction.
- A GitHub "DSS-Digital-Edition" repo is 0 bytes.
- `papyri/idp.data` (2.85 GB): CC BY 3.0 is project *documentation*; the license page returned an anti-bot wall and GitHub's detector shows null. Unverified — hold.

**Searched, not found — open follow-ups, not claimed:** Mill's actual 1707 apparatus · Aldine LXX 1518 · Alter 1786–87 · Birch 1788–1801 · Kipling's 1793 Bezae facsimile · Stephanus 1546/49/51 and Elzevir 1624 originals · Brescia 1494 · Buxtorf 1618–19 · Athias 1661 · **Paris Polyglot 1629–45** (and with it the Samaritan Pentateuch *editio princeps*, which exists only inside it — two leads behind Cloudflare bot-checks need a human browser) · Massuet's 1710 Irenaeus · Maurist Jerome (so **the pre-1820 route to Jerome on the Gospel of the Hebrews/Nazoreans is currently missing**) · Clementine 1592 / Sixtine 1590 Vulgate · Junius' 1665 Gothic · Bakar Georgian Bible 1743.

**No transcription exists anywhere:** the **Aleppo Codex** (c. 930 CE) — the best Masoretic manuscript — is page images only, everywhere. **Georgian** is the weakest-covered language in the corpus.

## §6 — Genuinely lost, stated flatly

- **Book of the Wars of the LORD** — one quoted line (Num 21:14). Nothing else ever existed to edit.
- **Book of Jasher** — two bare citations survive. The printed 1751 "Book of Jasher" (Jacob Ilive, "tr. by Alcuin") is a **confirmed 18th-century forgery**; it is in the manifest labeled as a forgery, not as a source.
- **Diatessaron** — no pre-1820 access is structurally possible: Arabic text first printed 1888, Ephrem's commentary translated 1876, the Dura fragment excavated 1933.
- **1 Enoch** — Laurence's first translation is **1821**, one year past the line. Legitimate workaround: **Syncellus's *Chronographia* (Goar, 1729)** preserves 1 Enoch 6–11 and 106–107 in Greek. And the Ge'ez text is already on disk.
- **Marcion's Gospel** and the **Gospel of the Ebionites** are the best-covered lost texts — both required patristic witnesses (Tertullian, Epiphanius) have verified pre-1820 editions in hand.
